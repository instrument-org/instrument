# Two sides prototype

The tab groups prototype rebuilt as a browser of two sides: every tab is a page with an address, a chat included, and a tab group shows one side of tabs or two. It follows the cofounders' 11-13 review of the tab groups prototype and the built-in apps wireframes. The build with one chat per group, its chat pinned to the right, is kept at the tag `tab-groups-one-chat-per-group` for comparison. Where this plan and `tab-groups-prototype.md` disagree, this one wins, and code of the earlier build that fights it goes.

Page contents can stay rough. What has to be right is the layout engine, dragging and dropping, and moving things around.

## The model

- A window lists its **tab groups** down the sidebar, in its own order, as the earlier build does.
- A group has **one or two sides**. A side is an ordered list of tabs with one tab up. One side of the group has the keyboard.
- **Every tab is a page with an address**: a website, a folder or a file in Files, a chat, a new tab (the zero state), or a built-in page (Settings, Memory, Skills, History, Activity, Apps). A chat is an ordinary tab: a group holds any number of chats, on either side, in any order, and nothing is pinned anywhere.
- Only two sides, ever. A third thing joins a side's tabs.
- **A side never shrinks to nothing.** The gutter between the sides resizes them and stops at each side's minimum width. Closing a side's last tab leaves the other side as the whole group.
- **Below the minimum width** the group's sides need, the group scrolls sideways instead of squeezing them.
- **Chats and tab groups are apart.** A group is a record of its own, under an id minted once, and a chat is what it always was. A chat's tab is a tab whose address names the chat, so a chat can have any number of tabs, in any number of groups, and nothing reads a chat out of a group's id or a group out of a chat. Opening a chat always opens it: no tab already on it is brought up in its place, and nothing refuses.
- A group's sidebar row shows the tabs that are up, on two lines: a group of one side its tab across the row, a group of two each side's tab in a half of equal width, with a hairline between. Each leads with its tab's mark and title, and its second line says where it is: a chat's latest line or step, a site, a file's folder, the part of a built-in page open, the words typed in a new tab. The whole row is one press, and under the pointer each half has an X, centered on its two lines, that closes its tab. A pin is the group's own, and a group has no name of its own.
- **A chat's state is its own.** Unread, working and waiting belong to the chat, never to a group, so every row and tab showing a chat shows its state, however many there are.
- **Closing a group never stops its chats.** A chat at work keeps working with no tab open, and its row is in History.
- **The sidebar** is tab groups and projects, with no search of its own. Chats are found from the Chats page, which ⇧⌘F opens with the caret in its search, from a chat's address field, and from a new tab's Closed recently list.
- **History** is every chat with no tab on this computer, and every kept draft no tab writes. Opening one from History opens a new group with that one tab, as today. Its rows, and the Chats page's, are a title over the chat's latest line, with no favicons.

## Opening things

- A click on any page but a chat opens in place, where Back returns, or as a new tab right after the tab it came from, in the same side, ⌘-click leaving it behind. Beyond what opens beside a chat (below), nothing opens in the other side unless the person asks for it: Open in Split, Open Link in Split, Move to Other Side, or a drag. Nothing already open hops to the other side by itself, and no card moves when something opens.
- **What a chat opens lands beside it**, placed against the chat's tab and never in its place: a link the person clicks in it, a page its agent opens (`agent-browser tab new`, a page it starts in), and what it shows (`instrument window show`). An agent's pages go against the chat's tab the person last sent a message from. Where (`besidePlacementOf` in `beside-chat.ts`):
  - Chat on the left, or alone, with nothing beside it (no other side, or one whose only tab is an unwritten New Tab, which the page replaces with no history behind it): the page becomes the right side, in front.
  - Chat on the left with a right side in use: the right side's last tab, in front unless the keyboard is in the right side, then behind.
  - Chat on the right: right after the chat, in its side. A click or `window show` brings it to the front; an agent's working page stays behind. Nothing opens to the left of a chat on the right.
  - ⌘-click, middle click and Open in New Tab go to the same place and never come to the front; with nothing beside the chat, the new right side shows anyway.
  - The agent's later pages go right after its previous one, in that side.
  - Which page types open beside them is one switch keyed by page type (`OPENS_BESIDE`), only a chat for now; built-in pages may join.
- An agent's page stays after the turn ends, until someone closes it. A chat with no tab, as one still at work after its group closed, gets a new group in the list with a tab on the chat, which the window does not move to. A blank page the agent starts in or asks for gets its tab only once it goes somewhere, so no blank tab shows in the strip. A tab the person opened stays theirs: the agent goes to another page in it only when asked to and given its id, and every other page it opens gets a tab of its own.
- **+ and ⌘T** always open a new tab (the zero state) after the tab up, in the side with the keyboard.
- **⌘N** always makes a new group: one side, one new chat, at the top of the list under New.
- **⇧⌘T** reopens what was closed last: a group closed whole comes back as that group, and a tab comes back where it was.
- **⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→** (Alt+Shift with the arrows off the Mac) move the keyboard to the left or right side, as code editors move between splits.
- **⌘K** is the launcher: a screen or a site picked there opens in the tab up on the side with the keyboard, where Back returns; a chat opens as a group of its own.
- **A chat asked for from outside** (a notification, the bell, an `instrument://chat` link from another app) comes up at its tab the person last sent from, wherever it is open; a chat with no tab opens as a group of its own. Clicks inside the app always open a new group.
- **Every tab's menu can open a chat beside it** (New Tab in Split), so a group of only websites is one click from the AI.

## The new tab, which is a new chat

A new tab is the chat zero state inside a browser's chrome: the toolbar with an empty address field, the bookmarks bar with Files first, and today's new-chat composer at the middle under them. Its tab, its row in the sidebar and the tab menu's New Tab in Split all call it New Tab. Typing an address in the field and pressing Enter goes there in this tab, and Back comes back to the new tab. Sending a message makes this tab the chat, in place. A file let go on the page opens in this tab, as a browser's new tab does, except over the composer, which attaches it to the first message; once something is written, the tab takes files as a chat's does. Each new tab is its own draft; one that is never typed in leaves nothing behind. Under the composer, Closed recently lists the five chats no window has a tab on that were active last, History's chats, each with its title and when it was last active (`closed-recently.tsx`). Choosing one opens it in this tab while nothing is written here, so Back comes back to the new tab, and as a tab right after it once something is.

## A side's chrome

Each side is a card of the window's, with its own head:

1. its row of tabs, ending in +;
2. its toolbar: back, forward, reload, then the address field, ending in the star;
3. the bookmarks bar, Files first, with the chevron beside Files that the earlier build added;
4. what the tab up shows.

A chat tab has the same chrome as any tab. Its address field reads as the Chat chip and its title; Back from a chat's first visit does nothing, and a page opened in place from a chat goes back to it. Under the bookmarks bar the chat's title leads its page, where its menu and Rename are, with the chat folder's button at the right, which opens the folder beside the chat. A task's tab has the same chrome, its address field reading as the chat's title, which goes back to the chat, then the task's.

Files keeps the same row as every tab, and its own controls (the sidebar, the view, the sort, search) sit in a second row under the bookmarks bar.

With two sides, the tabs of the side without the keyboard fade a little, and its half of the group's sidebar row is the narrower one.

**The address field** leads with an app chip for every built-in page and for a chat (Chat, Files, Settings, Memory, Skills, History, Activity, Apps), then where the tab is, in parts, the way a file's path reads: a chat's title, a part of Settings, what Activity shows and the row open in it. The chip goes to the app's index: Chat to every chat, Files to the home folder, and each built-in page to its start. Every part before the last is a place to step back out to, as a file's folders are. Both go in place, except from a chat's tab, where they open as a new tab right after it. A website shows its URL as today. Clicked, the field holds a website's URL or a file's or a folder's path to edit; the app's own places have no address a person types, so their field opens empty and searches the app the chip names, the way a browser's address bar searches a site once its keyword is in: Search chats, Search settings, Search activity, Search history, Search memories, Search skills, Search apps. Choosing a chat brings up its tab where it is open; otherwise it opens as History opens it, or as a new tab right after the chat from a chat's field. Choosing an entry of a built-in page goes there in place. The chip stays while the caret is in the field, and everything typed or pasted there searches its app, an address or a path included, so a link pasted in a chat's field searches the chats for it and opens nothing. Backspace in the empty field puts the chip away for the whole field, where an address opens, and Escape puts the place back. Only a click, ⌘L or a letter typed opens the field for typing: a caret the keyboard brings there leaves the place on show. Addresses for bookmarks and links use the link forms the app already opens (`instrument://chat/<id>`, `instrument://file/<path>`, `instrument://screen/<name>`), extended for the built-in pages, and the field never shows one to edit.

**The star** at the end of every address keeps the tab as a bookmark on the bookmarks bar, a chat's and a folder's included, and ⌘D does the same. Its card renames the bookmark or removes it.

**Bookmarks live on the bar.** Other browsers keep pinned tabs in their sidebar, one per row, and this sidebar is a tab group per row. A bookmark there would open in the tab up rather than as a row of its own, which mixes the two, so the sidebar keeps something by pinning its group.

## Built-in pages

Settings, Memory, Skills, History, Activity and Apps are tabs with addresses, opened from the address field by name, from the bookmarks bar, from the sidebar's corner (Settings) and from links. Each must work at half width: a built-in page narrower than 640px folds its own sidebar to a rail of icons, as Files folds its places. Each part of Settings has its own address. Memory and Skills leave Settings for pages of their own. History is the page the sidebar's History row opens. Settings open beside a chat while the agent changes a setting is the case to get right.

## Menus are the main way to make and manage splits

For now every split is made and managed from right-click menus, the same items wherever they apply:

- **A tab**: Open in Split (a copy on the other side) or Move to Other Side, New Tab in Split, Move to New Tab Group, Move to New Window, Duplicate, Bookmark, Close, Close Other Tabs, Close Tabs to the Left, Close Tabs to the Right. A chat's tab then has the chat's own items, each acting on that chat: its marks, Rename, Project, Copy (the chat folder's path, the work folder's path, for an agent), Send Feedback, Archive and Delete. A new chat's tab with words in it has Project, which files its draft.
- **A link** (in a page, in a chat, in a file shown in a tab): Open Link in Split, Open Link in New Tab, Open Link in New Tab Group, Open Link in New Window, Bookmark Link, Copy Link. A page's menu, and a file's, start with Open Link, which opens the link in place; a chat's has no plain Open, since a click there opens the link in a new tab beside the chat. A file or folder in Files, or a file chip in a chat, keeps a file's words (Open in Split, Add to Bookmarks).
- **A sidebar row**: Open in New Window, Duplicate, Pin, the Close items. Each acts on the group as a whole, whatever its tabs show; what acts on one chat is in that chat's tab menu.
- **A bookmark**: Open, Open in Split, Open in New Tab, Open in New Tab Group, Open in New Window, Rename, Delete.

⌘\ moves the tab up to the other side (Open in Split for a side's only tab). On-screen controls for splitting come later.

## Dragging and dropping, through one system

One drag system carries everything that can be held, and every place that takes a drop answers through it, so hover states, drop marks and what rides on the cursor agree everywhere.

- **Held**: a tab, a group (its sidebar row), a bookmark, and files (from Files or from the Finder).
- **Landings**:
  - a side's tab row, between two tabs;
  - a side's page: with one side, the left or right half lights and letting go makes that half a side holding the tab; with two, the side under the pointer lights and takes it as its last tab;
  - a sidebar row (joins that group) or the room between two rows (a new group there);
  - the bookmarks bar, between two bookmarks (keeps it as a bookmark there);
  - a web page, for files only: the page gets the files as if dropped from the Finder;
  - another window, and the open desktop (a new window).
- **Moves**: tabs within a side, between sides, between groups and between windows; rows within the list and between windows; bookmarks along the bar, off it (removed, with Undo in a toast) and back on; files onto tabs, rows, sides and web pages.
- **What shows on the way says what letting go does**, as soon as the pointer is there, with no wait for it to rest: tab-shaped over a tab row, with the others making room; nothing at the pointer over a row it joins, the row lit; a row standing in a room; the half or side it would take lit; a bookmark standing on the bar; off every window only, a picture of the tab's page, as the tab alone in a window of its own (a whole group's, of its window). From a drag's start, the ends of a lone side that can still split show faintly, each lighting its half once the pointer reaches it, so the split is found before it is needed. The earlier build's rules for rows, rooms and files stand, and the rest are made to match them.

## A chat knows the tabs around it

- The chat's agent works in the group of the chat's tab the person last sent a message from. It is told that group's layout as a terse block: each side's tabs in order, which is in front on each, which side has the keyboard, and which tab is this chat, with a line saying the tabs in front are most likely what the person is looking at.
- With it comes what changed since the layout it was told before: tabs opened, closed, moved within or between sides or groups, and navigated, and this chat's own moves.
- Reach is generous: the agent acts on any tab by its id, and lists any group by its id, by default the group it works in.
- Awareness is not a grant. A tab in the group opens nothing to the chat; only what the person attaches does. At the head of a chat's composer a quiet line says what its agent sees of the group, as "4 tabs, 3 files".

### How it's built

- **Parts in the chat's own log.** `data-groupLayout` (`KeptLayoutSchema`, `schemas/group-layout.ts`) is a diff part: written on the chat's own session only when its note (`groupLayoutModelNote`) or its group differs from the last layout the log has, so a page's title changing alone writes nothing, with where every other tab stood (`elsewhere`, tab id to group id), and never for a first layout that is the chat alone. `data-layoutChanges` goes beside it when there is a layout before it. `layoutParts` (`lib/group-layout-parts.ts`) decides both from the chat's records, so a part once written is never rewritten. They go on a person's message (`newMessage`) and on a wake that starts a turn (`deliver` in `wake-queue.ts`), never mid-turn.
- **The diff is pure.** `layoutChanges(before, after, elsewhere?)` in `lib/group-layout.ts` names closed and moved-out tabs first, then each tab of the new layout in order: opened or moved in, moved between sides, moved within its side (tabs outside the longest run that kept its order), navigated. A different group id is the chat moving groups, said alone. Groups are named by id. When one side becomes two, the side sharing more tabs goes on, the left on a tie; when two become one, the side that gave more tabs. An opened or closed tab an agent opened is marked `byAgent`, read from the tab's `openedBy`. `layoutChangesLine` is the person's line ("Opened Zendesk pricing beside this chat").
- **The window reads it.** `layoutReadOf` (`group-awareness.ts`) reads the group of a chat's tab through Core's `groupLayoutOf`: the tab is this chat, the keyboard is the side that has it, and every tab is named, a new chat as New Tab. A send carries it as `layout` on `message.create`, read by the sending tab (`shell.layoutContext(tabId)`), which also marks that tab as the one sent from (`promptedAt`). As the tabs settle, the window hands the workspace the read for every chat with a tab (`chats.layoutRead`), kept in memory, latest winning, for a wake.
- **The transcript** draws `data-layoutChanges` as one dim line over the person's message; the layout itself is a developer's note.
- **Checked by** `group-layout.test.ts` (the diff, `it.each` over a line notation), `group-layout-parts.test.ts` (when parts are written), `group-layout-model-text.test.ts` (the notes), `group-awareness.test.tsx` (the reading), the model-text byte snapshots, and the eval `group-layout-beside` (`layout` on a case or follow-up sends the read with no window behind it).

## Before merge

- **Archive goes in favor of closing.** A chat with no tab is in History, so closing its tabs puts it away the way Archive did. Archive stays in the chat's menus on this branch and comes out before the branch merges.

## Left open

Not built: projects beyond today's behavior, sidebar row rules beyond the basics, tasks as tabs, other AIs' sign-in doubling as model access, release notes and pages with actions, and the writing-tool idea.

## Tracks

The build is split into tracks that land on the prototype branch one at a time:

1. **Core**: the model (groups of one or two sides, every tab a page, a chat as an ordinary tab, any number of chats), the layout engine (sides, gutter, minimum widths, scrolling), the new chat as the new tab, opening rules, removing the pinned chat column and the earlier build's chat-first layout, and the tab menu's split items.
2. **Address**: app chips, a page's or a file's real address on the caret, the field's search in the app its chip names, the star and its menu, ⌘D, bookmarks of any address.
3. **Pages**: Settings as a tab with addressable parts, Memory, Skills, History, Activity and Apps as pages, folding at half width.
4. **Awareness**: the layout and its changes in each message, read by the agent, shown minimally.
5. **Drag**: one drag system for everything above.
6. **Menus**: the menus above everywhere they apply.

## How it's built

### Core

Paths are under `apps/studio/src/client/components/window/` unless named.

- **The model** (`tab-model.ts`): `WindowTabs` is one list of tabs across the window's groups, each naming its `group`, with `activeByGroup` (the tab up on the side with the keyboard) and `besideByGroup` (the tab up on the other side). A tab on the right side says `side: 1`, and `normalizeSides` turns a group with every tab on one side back into one side. `groupLayoutOf(state, group)` reads a group's sides, each its tabs in strip order and the one up, and which side has the keyboard; `useWindowTabs().layoutOf` is the same from React. `besideOf`, `sideIndexOf`, `chatTabOf` (the chat's tab with the latest `promptedAt`, else its first) and `chatUpIn` read the rest. A tab carries `openedBy`, the chat whose agent opened it, and on a chat's tab `promptedAt`, when the person last sent the chat a message from it. `groups` holds what each group keeps of its own: its pin and the left side's share of two.
- **Group ids are opaque.** A new group is `group:<uuid>`, and nothing reads a chat or a draft out of one. A group's address is `/chats?group=<id>`. `/chats/<id>` and `/chats?draft=<id>` asked of the window open that chat or draft as a group of its own: `openChat` and `openDraft` (`tab-groups.ts`).
- **Every tab is a page with an address.** A chat is a screen tab at `/chats/<id>` (`GroupChatScreen`, `chat-tab-screens.tsx`). A new tab is a new chat at `/chats` (`GroupNewChatScreen`), at `/chats?draft=<id>` once it has words, and sending walks that tab to the chat in place (`use-drafts.ts`). The composer offers no addresses: what is typed there is the first message.
- **Kept state** is local to this computer: `tab-groups.v2/<group>` in the layout file, one member per group, `{ active?, beside?, pinned?, share?, tabs }`, shaped so a later build could sync each as a synced store's member; each window's list is `tab-group-list.v2`. Nothing an earlier build kept is read. Upkeep (`upkeptList`, `tab-groups.ts`) gives a pinned group with no tabs a new chat, and lets an unpinned one go. Main reads the group a chat's agent works in (`keptWorkingGroupOfChat`) and the chat that opened a tab (`keptOpenerOfTab`) from the same records.
- **One function opens, one moves.** `openInGroup` and `windowTabs.openIn` place a tab by `{ after, group, side }` and `select`; `moveTabTo` and `windowTabs.moveTo` move one between sides, groups or places in a strip. `focusSide` gives a side the keyboard and `raiseTab` brings a tab up on its side. The caret goes with the keyboard on every path: the side's home takes it (`keyboard-home.ts`; a file's is its editor or its view), nothing on the other side takes it as it comes up, and a caret left there counts as on nothing, its selection dropped so dictation lands nowhere rather than in that side's composer. The openers (`use-openers.ts`) choose `after`: a click opens in place or right after its source on the same side; a chat's link right after the chat's tab, up, or behind on ⌘-click; + and ⌘T a new chat right after the tab up on the keyboard's side; ⌘N a new group; a page an agent opens right after the chat's tab, behind.
- **The layout engine** (`GroupSides`, `group-view.tsx`) draws a card per side, each with its own head: the strip and its + (`pane-tabs.tsx`), then the toolbar on every tab, chats included, and the bookmarks bar (`group-item.tsx`). Two sides split by a share kept per group, dragged at the gutter and snapping to half within `SIDE_SNAP_PX` (15). A side is never narrower than `SIDE_WIDTH_MIN` (440, wider than the layout research's 300, since a chat's composer and a page's toolbar both crowd below it); below that the group scrolls sideways, and the browser pool clips each guest to the scroller (`data-guest-clip`, `clipInset` in `client/lib/browser-pool.ts`). A page moving sides keeps its guest.
- **Closing.** A side's last tab closing leaves the other side as the group, the x on a one-sided group's last tab closes the group, and ⌘W there asks for a second press. Closing the window's last group makes a new one.
- **The composer's chip** is the tab up on the other side (`besideOf` into `chatContextItems`, `context-items.ts`): a page, a file or an app as before, and another chat as that chat, by its title. It goes with the message as what is in view and is never among the chips the chat is granted (`attached`, `send-context.ts`). A message's list of its group's tabs leaves out chat and new chat tabs.
- **Sidebar rows** (`group-sidebar.tsx`, `row-half.tsx`, `row-lines.ts`): `GroupRow` draws each side's tab up with `RowHalf`, and `useRowLines` says its two lines from the tab, the chat list and the drafts (`RowSources`, which a picture gives its own). A page's loading and failure come from its guest's load events. Starring a chat does not pin its group.
- **The tab menu** (`PaneTabMenu`, `pane-tabs.tsx`): Open in Split, Move to Other Side, New Tab in Split, Move to New Tab Group, Move to New Window, Duplicate, Close, Close Other Tabs, Close Tabs to the Left and to the Right.

### Where Core stands

- Checked in a dev instance: the zero state, a chat opened from History, New Chat in Split, an address typed into a new chat, Move to Other Side keeping the page, two chats side by side, and a narrow window scrolling sideways. Drags were driven only by synthetic events. Kept state was carried from v10 only from a fixture workspace set up in the earlier build, not from a real workspace.
- A chat's tab shows the bookmarks bar only while there are bookmarks, as a page's does, and a new chat always shows it, so the head changes height between them. A chat's toolbar has no reload.

### Address

Paths are under `apps/studio/src/`.

- **Addresses**: `addressOfTab(tab)` (`client/bookmarks/address.ts`) gives any tab's address: a site's URL, a file or folder as `instrument://file/<path>`, and anything else the app shows as `instrumentAddressOf(href)` in `shared/instrument-screens.ts` names it (a chat as `instrument://chat/<id>`, a built-in page as `instrument://screen/<name>`, a part of Settings as `instrument://settings/<part>`). `targetOfAddress` reads one back, and `sameAddress` compares two, so a trailing slash or an escaped character finds the same bookmark.
- **Opening**: `useOpenAddress()` (`client/bookmarks/open-address.ts`) opens any address here, in a new tab behind, on the other side of the group on screen, or as the first tab of a new tab group or a new window, and `openHowOf` reads which from a click (⌘ a new tab, ⇧ a new tab group). A screen a group's tab cannot stand at opens where the app keeps it.
- **The field**: `TabLocationRow` takes the tab's `address` and `title`. The chip is `AppChip` (`client/components/window/app-chip.tsx`), picked by `locationChip` in `tab-location.ts`; a new tab has none. With the caret the field holds `locationText`: a page's URL, a file's `file://` address, a folder's path, and nothing for the app's own places; `address` (`addressOfTab`) is only what the star keeps. In the app's own places the field searches the app its chip names (`fieldScopeOf` in `tab-location.ts`, the rows in `omnibar-scope.tsx`): the entries that match, best first, then the page's own search for the words where the page keeps one in its address (`?q=`). A press on the field and ⌘L open it for typing through `editField` (`omnibar-field.ts`); focus arriving any other way leaves the place on show until a letter is typed. The general field (a new tab, a page, a file, a folder, or a field whose chip was put away) opens a typed `instrument://` address on Enter, and offers built-in pages (`builtInPages()` in `shared/instrument-screens.ts`, each part of Settings among them), chats and the app's own bookmarks as rows from two letters of a name, first when the name is typed whole.
- **The star**: `BookmarkStar` (`bookmark-star.tsx`) and its card. ⌘D is the window command `bookmarkTab`, in the Bookmarks menu, and `requestBookmarkTab()` presses the visible star with the most ancestors in common with the focused element, so each side's row answers for its own side. A code editor keeps ⌘D.
- **Bookmarks** are on the bar (`bookmarks-bar.tsx`), which opens them through `bookmarkClicks` and `BookmarkContextMenu` (`bookmark-menu.tsx`), each wearing its tab's mark (`AddressMark`). A stored bookmark that names a place (`where`) reads as one on the bar at its order, since `electron-main/bookmarks/bookmark-store.ts` reads only `order`, `title` and `url`.
- **For other tracks**: `useBookmarkActions()` (`client/bookmarks/actions.ts`) gives `bookmark({ title, url })`, `isBookmarked(url)`, `bookmarkOf(url)`, `rename` and `remove`, and re-exports `addressOfTab`. A tab's or a link's Bookmark item is `bookmark({ title, url: addressOfTab(tab) })`.
- **One mapping**: which built-in page an href is at and its address are `screenOfHref` and `instrumentAddressOf` in `shared/instrument-screens.ts`; `address.ts` keeps only the tab-level layer over them: sites, files and folders.

### Pages

- **Every built-in page is a row of `SCREENS`** (`shared/instrument-screens.ts`): its name, title, address and what it is for, which the product guide lists for the agent. `instrumentAddressOf` gives a tab's `instrument://` address from it: `instrument://screen/<name>` for Activity, Apps, Files, History, Memory, Skills and the rest, `instrument://settings/<part>` for a part of Settings. `screenOfHref` reads an address back to its page and part, `builtInPages()` lists every page and part of Settings with its address for the field's rows, and `ScreenMark` (`app-chip.tsx`) is each page's mark, so the tab strip, the chip, a row and a bookmark name a page the same way.
- **Settings is a tab** at `/settings/<part>`, one address per part in `SETTINGS_SECTIONS` (`general`, `account`, `providers`, `permissions`, `storage`, `activity`, `developer`), and `/settings` goes to General. All of Settings is one tab (`tabIdentity`), so asking for it brings the one open forward and walks it to the part asked for (`bringForwardShowing` in `built-in-pages.tsx`). A name that is not a part (`/settings/theme`) is a setting's link, which the route sends to the setting's part with its row lit. What an address asks of the page once (a row to light, a memory to show, the account's usage, the diagnostic log) the page takes and drops from its address (`useArrivingAsk`), so going back never asks it again; the search's words stay in it as `q`.
- **Opening a page.** `openSettings` and `openBuiltInPage` put a request in `pageRequestAtom`, and each window's `BuiltInPageOpener` brings the page forward in the group up, or opens it as a new tab after the tab up. The sidebar's Settings, ⌘,, links into Settings, the command menu's Settings, Memory, Skills and History rows, the sidebar's History row and ⌘Y all go through it. There is no Settings dialog.
- **Memory and Skills** are pages of their own (`/memory`, `/skills`): the sections Settings had, in `BuiltInPage`. Settings' search still finds them and opens them in its tab, and their old links (`instrument://settings/memory`, `memory-import`, `new-skill`) open them.
- **History** is `/history`, its search in `q` (`HistoryPage`): every chat with no tab on this computer and every kept draft no tab writes, by day, each a title over its latest line. Opening one opens it as a group of its own and the window moves to it, leaving the History tab where it was. With `show=chats` it is every chat, open or not, titled Chats, its search box Search chats: the index Chat's chip goes to (`CHATS_INDEX_HREF`), one tab of its own beside History (`history:chats` in `tab-identity.ts`).
- **The chip and the parts** (`tab-location-row.tsx`): the chip goes to `appIndexHref(app)` (`app-chip.tsx`) through the row's `onVisit`, which walks a screen's tab in place and opens after a chat's tab. A built-in page's location carries the `part` shown (a part of Settings, Activity's filter, with `partHref`) and the `item` open in it (Activity's open row, from `useTabSurface("item", …)`), and `locationCrumbs` makes the part a crumb to `partHref` while an item is open. A part of an app page goes through `onVisit` too, since the window keeps one tab per built-in page and would only bring it up as it is.
- **Half width.** Under `PAGE_FOLD_PX` (640), measured on the page itself (`useIsNarrower`), a page folds what it keeps beside its content. Settings' sidebar becomes a rail of the parts' icons, each naming itself on hover, its search opening over the part from the rail's magnifier. Activity's details open under the row picked, and its search and filters go under its title. Memory, Skills and History are one column whose padding follows a container query. Apps already lays out by container query, and Files folds its places at its own 576px.
- **Live values.** Every part reads its preferences and lists through live queries, so Settings beside a chat shows a setting changed elsewhere as it changes.

### Menus

Paths are under `apps/studio/src/client/` unless named.

- **One list of places.** `useWindowPlaces()` (`hooks/use-open-target.tsx`) gives the rows every link and file menu has after its own Open: Open in Split, Open in New Tab, Open in New Tab Group, Open in New Window. `useBookmarkRow()` adds Add to Bookmarks for an address, which only adds: a second time it says the address is already in Bookmarks. A link in text (`ExternalLink`, an app link's chip, `LinkSurface`) asks with `isLink`, which words the rows as a web page's menu does (Open Link in Split, Bookmark Link) and leaves its menu without the plain Open, except in `LinkSurface`, where a click opens in place. The OS's menu shows the list through `showOpenMenu`, and a menu the app draws (a file link in a chat, a Files row, a bookmark) shows the same rows with `PlaceItems` (`components/window/place-items.tsx`), which gives each its icon.
- **Opening into a place.** `OpenOptions` (`components/window/context.ts`) takes `into` (`split`, `group`, `window`) and `from`, the tab that asked, which surfaces pass on through `ownPlaceOf`. `openInto` in `components/window/use-openers.ts` places it: a split is the other side of the asking tab's group, right after the tab up there, made when the group has one side; a new tab group is `newGroup`; a new window is a group made without showing it here, then moved to a window of its own. Shift-click on a link or a file is Open in New Tab Group, and a chat opened into a place is a tab of its own there.
- **A web page's menu** is built in main (`LINK_PLACES` in `electron-main/browser-view/guest-interactions.ts`): Open Link in Split, Open Link in New Tab, Open Link in New Tab Group, Open Link in New Window, then Bookmark Link. A place goes to the window as `browser.open-in-new-tab` with its `into`, and Bookmark Link as `browser.bookmark-link`, which `use-openers.ts` keeps with `useAddBookmark` (`bookmarks/actions.ts`), named by the link's text.
- **Files** (`components/window/computer-page.tsx`): the list, columns and icons views share one row menu. `placesOfOne` gives a single selected item Open in Split, Open in New Tab Group and Add to Bookmarks; with several selected the menu keeps Open in New Tabs and Open in New Window.
- **A tab's menu** (`components/window/tab-menu-items.tsx`) is Core's, with Add to Bookmarks or Remove from Bookmarks after Duplicate for a tab with an address (`pane-tabs.tsx`), and ⌘\ shown on the tab up's first row. After a divider, a chat's tab has `ChatTabItems` and a new chat's tab with a draft has `DraftTabItems` (`components/window/chat-tab-menu.tsx`), each given its own chat or draft, so New project… files that one and not the chat the window has up.
- **A bookmark's menu** (`components/window/bookmark-menu.tsx`): Open, then `BOOKMARK_PLACES` through `useOpenAddress` (`OpenHow` has `split` and `newWindow`), then Rename, Copy Address and Delete.

- **The side chord**, ⌘\, is `moveTabToOtherSide` (`shared/window-shortcuts.ts`), in the Tab menu and the shortcut guide. ### Drag

Paths are under `apps/studio/src/client/components/window/` unless named.

- **One session** (`drag/session.ts`): a window has at most one drag, whatever holds it: the pointer (a tab, a sidebar row, a bookmark), the system's drag (files from the Finder, Files or a file card), or main, for another window's tab or row held over this one. What is held is a `Held` and what letting go would do a `Landing` (`drag/types.ts`); `dragAtom` is what the window draws from, and `dragPoint` is the pointer, read without drawing again. Every press becomes a drag past `START_PX` (8) through `pressToDrag`, and Escape, the window losing focus and a canceled pointer all end it in one place. A page runs in a process of its own that takes every move over it, and the release, from a press in the window, so pages let the pointer pass from the press on (`data-pressing`, then `data-tab-held` for the drag, `globals.css`): without it a bookmark pulled straight down onto the page under the bar never moved far enough in the window to become a drag. A move with the button up ends a press whose release the window never heard. A page with the keyboard hears the window's keys and its loss of focus in a process of its own (on a Mac the window's `document.hasFocus()` reads false meanwhile), so the press and the drag hold pages off the keyboard (`holdKeyboardFromPages` in `browser-pool.ts`), and the page a press brought up takes it once the press ends as a click or the drag ends. A page that takes it anyway blurs the window with the page as `document.activeElement`, which ends nothing: the drag takes the keyboard back and puts it home after. A release's click is swallowed.
- **Every place that takes a drop registers a target** (`useDropTarget`): where it takes drops, what letting go at a point does (a landing, or `"native"` where only the system's drag can hand files over), a list that scrolls near its ends, and the places it shows faintly from the drag's start (`hints`, published as `Drag.hints`). The session measures once a frame, the smallest target under the pointer first, so a strip answers before the page around it. The targets are each side's strip (`useStripTarget`, `drag/use-group-drag.ts`), a group's side pages (`useSidePagesTarget`), the sidebar (`useSidebarTarget`, over `sidebar-drop.ts`), and the bookmarks bar (`useBookmarksTarget`, `drag/use-bookmarks-drag.ts`).
- **One lander** (`useLander` in `drag/use-drag-system.ts`) does what letting go does, through Core's moves and openers and the bookmarks' ops: a tab moves; files and a bookmark open as tabs at the same places, a bookmark staying kept, as Open in Split leaves it; a row takes its place in the list; a tab or a bookmark let go on the bookmarks is kept there (`useKeepBookmark`), and a tab whose address is kept already moves that bookmark there, as an address is kept once; a bookmark let go where nothing takes it is removed, with Undo in a toast (`useRemoveBookmarkWithUndo`).
- **A side's page** (`sideZoneAt`, `drag/landing.ts`): with one side, a band at each end (`splitBands`, `splitBandWidth`, a fifth of the page from 120 to 360px) lights that half the moment the pointer is in it and makes it a side, for anything held but the side's only tab; with two sides, the side under the pointer lights and takes the tab as its last. Nothing waits for the pointer to rest: a half lit while a tab only passes through still says what letting go there does. The margin between an outer page and the window's edge is that page's end (`pagesReach`, for a page within 24px of the edge, and on past the edge as far as the pointer is still in the window), so a tab flung to the window's edge splits too. Files land on a side's page as a tab does, and open there as its last tabs: an open file's editor is part of its page, so nothing let go over it goes into the file. A site takes them natively, and so do a chat's composer and a folder in Files (`data-file-drop-region`) away from a lone side's ends, and a new tab up turns into them.
- **What shows on the way** comes from the target the drag is over, never from two places at once. A strip stands what is held among its tabs as tabs, the first at the pointer (`useSideStrip`, drawn by `tab-strip.tsx`); the sidebar lights a row or opens a room (`sidebar-drop.ts`); the bookmarks stand what is held where it would be kept (`useShownBookmarks`), a tab under its address and title or as the bookmark its address is kept as, the bookmark that moves gone from its old place. `drag/drag-layer.tsx` draws the rest: a lone side's ends, faint from the drag's start (`DROP_HINT_TONE`), each fading out as the half it makes lights; the half or side lit, sliding from one to the next; and where nothing in this window lands, a chip at the pointer, or the row for a whole group. A bookmark's chip is faded, as letting go there removes it. Lit places share one tone (`drag/tone.ts`) and none is green.
- **Nothing waits to show.** Two waits stay, since each moves things under the pointer, and what they lead to is lit at once meanwhile: a folder in Files springs open after the drag stays on it `SPRING_DELAY_MS` (700, `spring-loading.ts`), lit as the place files go from the first move over it; and a list near its ends eases its scrolling in over 400ms (`scrollSpeed`).
- **Past the window** (`useCarrier`): a group's tab starts main's carry at its first movement, so its picture is ready as it leaves; a row's starts once the row leaves the window. Off every window the tab's strip closes up behind it and the tab beside it comes up, and main shows the picture; let go there, it is a group of its own in a new window. Over another window the picture hides (`shownPhase`, `electron-main/windows/tab-carry.ts`) and that window measures it as its own drag (`useRemoteDrags`), telling main where it lands (`GroupTabLanding`, `shared/tabs.ts`, which has a strip's side and a side's page): a strip, a side's page, a sidebar row or a room. A window's only tab, or only group, takes its window along. Let go over a window that takes it nowhere, a tab goes back, and a window going along stays there.
- **Files held by the system** (`useSystemFileDrags`) carry only their count until let go, so main reads the system's drag pasteboard (`files.dragged`) to name them. Until it answers only the landing shows, and off the Mac they are named only once let go. A drag lands only where a target takes it, and anywhere else goes on to what is under it: Files' folders, a composer, the window's last resort.
- **Replaced**: `file-drops.ts`, `use-tab-carry.ts` and most of `tab-drag.ts`, the earlier build's separate drags for files, carrying and the strip.
- **Driving one**: `.agents/skills/studio-chrome-devtools/scripts/drag-drive.mjs` drives a drag with real mouse input, or files through the system's drag, and reports `window.__studioDrive.drag()` (`client/lib/studio-drive.ts`) at each stop, with shots, frame bursts, and other windows watched.
- **Checked by** `drag/landing.test.ts` (the zones, the bands shown from the start, the margin past the page, leaving the window, scrolling), `drag/session.browser.test.tsx` (the press, the keyboard, a page's end lit at once), `tab-strip.browser.test.tsx`, `sidebar-drop.browser.test.tsx` and `electron-main/windows/tab-carry.test.ts`.
