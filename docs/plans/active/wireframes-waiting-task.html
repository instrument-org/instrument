A task that is waiting on you

The risk in letting the agent ask questions: someone presses go, walks away, and comes back to a task that has been paused for two hours. Frame 1 is the failure. Frames 2 and 3 are the two ways out, which are not mutually exclusive. Frame 4 is the return. Nothing here is built.

A blocked turn is invisible from anywhere except the task it happened in. This is the reason not to ship the asking mechanism enabled by default.

3 It gives up on its own
Asked 40 minutes ago

If nothing is answered, the agent stops waiting and writes down what it could not do.

Two hours later

Stopped: needed a Linear sign-in. Everything else is done. Pick this back up whenever.

Cheap, and honest. But a user who did intend to answer comes back to a task that quit on them, which reads as the product giving up.

Expiry turns an indefinite pause into a finished turn with a legible reason. Whether that feels considerate or abandoning is the open question, and it is worth asking real users.

4 Coming back

Sign in to Linear

Not now Open sign-in

Survived a quit and relaunch. The half-filled form would too, because the question lives in the task record rather than in memory.

The question is still answerable and says how long it has been waiting. Durability is what makes either policy above viable; without it, both are guesses about a state that was lost.

Frames 2 and 3 are alternatives to decide between, not a sequence, and they can also be combined: surface it, and expire it much later. Today neither is possible, because the pending state lives in machine memory and does not survive a restart at all. That is the prerequisite under all of this.
