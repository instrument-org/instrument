Status: open.

# Tab groups prototype

A throwaway prototype of a new window layout, on its own branch, for the two cofounders to use before anything about it is decided for the product. It replaces the current shell; it does not sit beside it behind a setting.

## The layout

- A left **sidebar of tab groups** replaces the app rail, the chat list and the window tab strip.
- The open tab group's **tabs** head the work's card, as a browser's do: plain rounded tabs on the card's own ground, the tab up a pill lifted off it. After them comes +. The **chat tab** is named for the chat: its title, cut short to fit, or "New chat" until it starts, after the chat bubble, which gives way to the app's working dots while the agent works, and it ends in an X. It stands where the chat is: the tab up, first on the tab bar, while the chat is the whole group, and the head of the chat's card while that card is open. Its X closes the chat's card, and the work takes the whole group, its tabs ending in the chat tab, dim as a tab not up is, where a press opens the card again. While the chat is the whole group its X closes the group, as the X on a window's last tab closes the window.
- **No second title.** The chat's head is gone: the chat tab is the only place its title shows. The head's menu is the chat tab's, on a right-click anywhere on it, and it has the head's folder button as its first item, Show Chat Folder.
- **Chat first.** A new group, and a group whose last tab closed, is its chat alone, one card laid out as a chat site in a browser: a tab bar a shade darker than the toolbar, the chat tab first on it, lifted off it as the tab up is, and + after it; under it the toolbar a page has, with back, forward and reload turned off, and the bookmarks bar under it, always shown here, then the chat. The bookmarks bar leads with Files, which opens Files where it last stood as a tab of the group, and so moves the chat to its card. A chevron beside it lists the group's project folder and the places the Files sidebar keeps, to open Files at one of them. The address field works as it does on a page, and Enter opens what it names as a tab in this group, which moves the chat to a card of its own. The composer has the cursor. A short line typed into the composer is offered as an address or a search with the address field's own rows over the box, less the search engine's suggestions: Enter opens an address, the arrows pick a search or a page, and Enter on plain words sends them.
- **A new chat** opens as 1.0's did, under that toolbar: the composer at the middle, the Instrument mark and "How can I help?" over it, and a pale glint across the composer as the chat arrives. No title and no project chips, and for now no way to file it under a project before it starts. From the first message on it is the transcript, with the composer at its foot.
- **The chat moves aside** once a tab opens, whether a page, Files or a new tab: it goes to a card of its own on the right, beside the work's card, narrow (300px, the pane's floor) until the gap between the cards is dragged. It keeps its width as the window or the sidebar changes, and the work takes the difference. Closing the last tab makes the chat the whole group again. Nothing opens a tab the person didn't ask for. The composer's context follows the tab up: a page or a file up is its chip, and a new tab adds none.
- Under the tabs, a **toolbar**: back, forward, reload, then the address field. No dividers between them. It belongs to the work's card.
- **Two cards**: while the chat has its card, the work and the chat are two cards side by side, rounded as the window's card is, with the window's ground between them as wide as it is at the window's edges. A drag in that gap resizes them. Each card has its own head: the work's its tabs, their + and the toolbar, the chat's the chat tab across its top. Their empty space drags the window. While the chat's card is closed the work's card takes the row and its tabs end in the chat tab. On Windows and Linux the caption buttons come after whichever head reaches the top right.
- **A split of two panes.** The two cards are a split that knows nothing of the chat: two panes, each a card with its own head. The chat stays on the right at its width, and a split of two pages, dragging a tab into one side, swapping sides and even halves are left for later.
- **No Ask.** With the chat beside everything, nothing offers to ask about a page, a file or a folder in a new chat: no Ask in the toolbar, in Files or in a menu, and no Convert…. Ask on a selection, which marks it for the chat beside it, stays.

The look is the app's current colors, type and components. Nothing is restyled beyond what the move needs.

## Scope: structural only

Build the structure, and nothing that doesn't already exist in the product. The chat is today's chat with its existing behavior: composer, model picker, transcript, asks, tasks. No suggestions, no generated follow-ups, no new AI features. No AI names a group: a group's title is the name the person typed for it, else its chat's title, or for a group whose chat has not started, its words or the tab it has up. AI keeps naming chats.

## Tab groups

- One chat per tab group. A group is a chat plus its tabs, so the chat's existing records are the group's records, and the tabs a chat already keeps (today's side pane tabs) are the group's tabs.
- **Sidebar rows** are two lines: the title, then the chat's latest line (the step it is on, the question it waits on, or its last reply), with the tabs' favicons at the line's right end in under half its width. A group whose chat has not started shows its tabs' favicons alone. A new tab has no favicon there. No time and no chat icon on rows; the pointer on a row brings up its close X at the title line's end. Unread groups have a bold title. Starred becomes pinned: pinned rows sit above a plain divider. A group whose chat has not started is pinned by a pin of its own, from its menu or by dragging it among the pinned rows, and when its chat starts the chat is starred, which keeps it pinned.
- **Names**: any group can be named by hand, typing in place on its row, from a double click on its title or the row menu's Rename. Renaming lives in the sidebar only: the tab bar has no group rename, and the chat tab's menu still renames the chat. A group's name is its own, for every group, a chat's or not: the row shows the typed name over the default, the chat's title for a chat's group and the derived title otherwise, and clearing the name returns the default. A typed name never becomes the chat's title, and the chat's title never overwrites a typed name.
- **Duplicate Tab Group**, in a row's menu, makes a copy just below the group and shows it: the same tabs in the same order with the same one up, a page at its current address without its back history, a screen (Files, a file, a settings screen) at its current address, and a new tab as a new tab. The copy is a new group whose chat has not started, filed under the same project, with the group's typed name. The conversation is not copied.
- **New**: a "New" row with + at the top of the list, under the pinned rows and their divider, showing ⌘N. New groups land right under it, newest first, so the list grows away from it. The list scrolls whole, New with it.
- **Projects** are listed at the top of the sidebar with no count. Opening one, from its row or the command menu, takes the sidebar over as today, opening the sidebar first when it is closed, with its banner, its groups and a back control, sliding in from the right and back out from the left, with the sidebar washed in the project's color. New project is a near-hidden + (or the right-click menu) on the Projects heading.
- **The sidebar's top row**: the sidebar toggle right after the traffic lights on a Mac and first on Windows and Linux, the notifications bell at the row's right end. On a Mac the traffic lights go with the sidebar: while it is closed they are hidden, and the toggle moves into their corner, 12px in, lined up with the tab bar's tabs, as it stands on Windows and Linux. They come back as the sidebar opens, with no slide, since the system draws them. No Activity button.
- **The sidebar's footer**: the account and Settings at the bottom left, with a Help menu (a ? button) that holds feedback, laid out for the sidebar's width rather than the old thin rail.
- **Search** sits at the top of the list, full width, showing ⇧⌘F. It shows this window's groups first, then the chats other windows have open, then History, each below a line. It finds a chat by what the chat list's search reads on its row (the title, the first message, the latest line, the project, and the files, sites and apps it used), and a group whose chat has not started by its words, its project and its tabs' page titles. When the words find nothing, the decision model reads the chats' titles for the ones meant, as the chat list does, under AI Results. History is a dim row at the list's end, past the groups, that names none of what it holds and opens the History view (⌘Y does too). History lists every chat and kept draft that no window has open, most recent activity first, archived chats included; opening one is an explicit action and adds it at the top of the list, under New.
- Each window has its own tab groups, in its own order. A group is open in one window at a time: choosing one that another window has open, from History, search, a notification, Go to or a chat link, brings that window forward. A chat no window has open stays in History, its row showing when it is working or waiting.
- On first launch, a window starts with one new tab group and no chats open. Every existing chat is in History, found from there or from search. Nothing on disk changes for this.

## Tabs

- **⌘N** makes a new tab group, **⌘T** a new tab in the open group. ⌘N is "New chat" today. The command menu's New chat and New tab rows do the same.
- **Go to Chat** (⌃⌘1) opens the group's chat column when it is closed and puts the keyboard in the chat's composer.
- Every tab behavior and shortcut works as today's window tabs do: ⌘-click opens a link in a background tab in this group, middle-click and close, reopen closed tab, the tab menu, shrinking tabs, status marks, tooltips. Shift-click opens in a new tab group.
- A page the agent opens itself is a tab in its group.
- A file or link opened from outside the app opens as a new tab group at the top of the list.
- **Dragging tabs**: what shows on the way says what letting go does. Over the middle of another group's row, the row takes a quiet fill with a hairline and the tab moves into that group, with nothing drawn at the pointer. Near the line between two rows, or a row's outer edge at the top or bottom of the pinned rows or the rest, the rows part to make room, as tabs do in a strip, and the tab stands in that room as the row of the group it would become; let go, it is that group, there, pinned among the pinned rows. Over New it stands at the top of the unpinned rows, and over the empty list under the rows, at their end. The band near each line is a few px into each row, so the middle of a row is easy to hit and rows have no extra room between them at rest. Over the sidebar where it lands nowhere (its search, the divider, History), the tab goes as a one-line row at the pointer, its mark and title, and over the strip it is a tab at its place. The picture of a window shows only where letting go makes a window. The same works over another window's sidebar, and tearing a tab out into its own window works as today. Nothing lights in green, and no line marks a room.
- **Dragging rows**: a row dragged within the list lifts out of it, the other rows opening a room exactly its height where it would land, with no line. Dropped above the pinned divider it is pinned, below it unpinned. Dragged out of the window it becomes a window of its own, and over another window's sidebar it moves there into a room the same way. A window's only group drags as a window's only tab does on main: the window goes with the pointer, and once the group lands in another window's sidebar, its window closes.
- **Dragging files in**: a file or folder dragged into the window, from Files or from anywhere else, lands where a tab let go at the same spot does. On a row's middle it joins that group; in a room between rows, over New or over the empty list it becomes a group of its own there; over the tab strip it becomes a tab of the group up, at the place it is let go; over a new tab, that tab becomes it. A file opens as Files opens it, a folder as Files at that folder, and several make a tab each. It looks the part on the way, as a tab does: the row it would join lights, the row it would make stands in its room with the marks of every file, and over the strip it is a tab at the pointer, the others making room. The composer still attaches a file let go over it, and anywhere nothing takes it, it opens as a group of its own.

## The new tab

⌘T or + opens one in any state, a browser's new tab that looks the same everywhere:

- The toolbar a page has, back, forward and reload before the address field, and the bookmarks bar under it. The field has the cursor as the tab opens, finishes addresses, lists pages from history and the search engine's suggestions, and searches the web, as it does on a page.
- Under them, the Files sidebar's places down the page, a list centered as a block, as Finder's sidebar lists them: a small dim heading, then a row of icon and name for each. Under "Project", the group's project folder when it is filed under one; under "Places", the workspace, then home, Desktop, Documents and Downloads as Pinned has them. Not This Mac or the drives. Each turns the tab into Files there. The places have no keys. No mark, no glint, no field of its own.
- No composer of its own: the chat's composer, in its card, is the one place to type to the chat. Until the chat starts, what is typed there is the group's draft.
- Sending the first message from a new group starts its chat in the same group. Its pages and Files tabs stay; a new tab closes, so a group that had only a new tab goes back to chat first.
- The tab strip's + is plain: no menu, no extra buttons beside it, no key shown. While the group has no tabs it is "+ New tab", the one tab-shaped thing on an empty strip.

## Shortcut keys shown

Keys show only on the sidebar's New row, in the search field, and as ⌘L in the address field of a group that is its chat alone, while the field is empty and does not have the cursor: the composer has it there, so the key says how to reach the field. Nowhere else: never beside an icon button, never on +, never in a page's or a new tab's address field.

## Closing

- ⌘W closes the current tab. A group's last tab closes like any other, however it closes, and the chat comes back to the center. ⌘W while the chat fills the center closes the group, with a hint to press it again.
- Closing a group whose agent is running asks first. The dialog is small and terse: a copy of the group's sidebar row, then "Stop and close" (Return) and "Keep open" (Esc), with those keys shown.
- A row's menu closes the groups around it the way a tab's menu does on main: Close Tab Group, then Close Other Tab Groups, Close Tab Groups Above and Close Tab Groups Below, each hidden when it would close nothing. Pinned rows stay open through them, as a browser's pinned tabs do. When any of the groups closing has a running agent, the dialog asks once for all of them, with a copy of each one's row.
- Closing a group takes it out of the window and nothing more: its chat is not archived and goes back to History. A group whose chat has not started is kept in History when it has words, files, tabs (a new tab aside), a name or a pin, so a named group of dropped files works as a bookmark; one with none of these is thrown away.

## Window controls on Windows and Linux

Roughed in: the caption buttons go at the top right as today, and what sits there moves left to make room, the way the layout moves for the macOS traffic lights. The collapsed chat's corner is the hard case; a rough answer is enough for this version.

## Not building

- A hover peek of the collapsed sidebar.
- More than one chat per group, the tab overview grid, suggestions of any kind, the new tab's inbox and clipboard rows.
- Any change to the workspace folder on disk.
- A preview build. The prototype runs locally until it has been used and revised.

## Order of work

1. The shell: the sidebar of tab groups, the tabs over the center, the toolbar, the chat column and its collapse, ⌘N and ⌘T, the new tab page.
2. Closing, History and search.
3. Dragging tabs between groups and into the sidebar.
4. The sidebar's top row and footer, projects, and the Windows and Linux controls roughed in.

## How it's built

- **A tab group is a chat's tab group as the code already has it.** The window's tabs are grouped by `group` in `window-tabs.ts` (the `layout` kept file, shared by every window): a chat's group is its `ChatId`, a new chat's is its draft's (`draftGroupOf`). A tab group is one of those groups, with its chat in the card on the right. A group whose chat has not started is a draft's group: the chat's card there is today's new chat composer, and its first message starts the chat and hands the draft's tabs to it as they stand, less its new tabs (`handOverTabs`). A new group (`make` in `tab-groups.ts`) opens no tab unless something is to open in it.
- **The window has one app tab.** The app tab strip and the rail are gone. Each window keeps one app tab (`appTabsAtom`), standing on the open group's address (`/chats/<id>`, or `/chats?draft=<id>` for a group whose chat has not started); choosing a row in the sidebar moves that tab there. A window with no tab groups of its own starts on one new group, whatever app tabs it kept before. Everything else a window shows stands in a group: Apps, Activity, release notes, extensions, Show in Folder, a settings link, a file or a page reached at the window level opens as a tab in the group up, or in a new group when none is (`useTabGroupUpkeep`). Only the debug pages still take the app tab.
- **A group is a split of two cards.** `SplitView` (`split-view.tsx`) lays out two panes, each a card of the window's (`WINDOW_CARD`) with its own head over its body, the window's gap (`WINDOW_CARD_GAP`) between them and a `ResizeHandle` in it. The start pane flexes; the end pane is sized by the share it keeps (`chatShareAtom`, with its 300px floor in `atoms/right-pane.ts`) and holds its width as the row changes. The end pane is put away, open, or the whole row (`endStands`), and each pane's body stays where it is in the tree whichever it is, so the pages in it survive. Nothing in the split is the chat's. While a group is shown it is marked `data-own-cards`, and the window's own card stands back to its bounds: no ground, shadow or clip.
- **The work is the start pane, the chat the end pane.** `PaneTabs` draws the group's tabs across the work card's head, the side pane's tabs as they were, on the card's ground with the tab up on `--card`, and `GroupItem` draws the tab up under its address row. The sidebar toggle is the window frame's `corner`, laid over the window's top left, and whichever row reaches there while the sidebar is closed leaves it room. The chat's card is `ChatScreen`, or `NewChatView` for a group whose chat has not started, under `ChatTab` (`chat-tab.tsx`) as its head; `stands` places the chat tab as the tab up while the chat is the whole group, first on the tab bar (`PaneTabs`' `lead`), or the head of its open card. Its X sets the card closed (`setChatOpen`), or closes the group (the shell's `closeGroup`) while the chat is the whole group. The chat tab's menu is `ChatMenuItems` from `chat-header.tsx`, drawn through `MenuComponents` as a context menu on a right-click. The chat-first tab bar's look is the `group-strip` utility: its own darker ground, with the chat tab lifted off it on `--tab-selected`. `BookmarksBar` leads with Files where it is given `files`: on every bar a group draws, its chevron's places from `useFilesPlaces`, which the new tab's places list reads too. The new tab is the `/new-tab` screen in a group (`isHomeTab`), drawn by `GroupItem` under the toolbar a page has (`TabLocationRow`, its field taking the caret as the tab comes up) as `GroupNewTab`, the places list. A group with no tabs is chat first: `GroupView` lets the chat's card fill the split (`fills`) under a tab bar of `ChatTab` and +, a `TabLocationRow` standing on `{ kind: "newTab" }` with its arrows off, and the bookmarks bar, keeping one tree for it so the composer keeps its words as the chat moves, and opens the chat's card again when it was closed. The chat-first composer's rows are `useComposerAddress`, around the omnibar's `useOmnibarRows` without the engine. A draft's composer attaches only the tab up (`draftItems` in `send-context.ts`). `closeTab` in `tab-model.ts` leaves a group with no tabs.
- **New state** is per device and lives in the `view` kept file: whether the sidebar is open, which groups have their chat's card closed, the names typed for groups, by group, chats' and drafts' alike (`groupNamesAtom`), and the pins of groups whose chat has not started (`groupPinsAtom`). When a group's chat starts, its name goes on under the chat's id (`nameCarried`) and its pin becomes the chat's star; a draft's group thrown away takes its name and pin with it (`forgetGroupAtom`). A draft's group with tabs, a name or a pin is kept whatever its draft holds (`keptDraftGroupsAtom`), and since main writes a draft only once it has words or files, a window makes those drafts again, empty, before it first draws (`restoreKeptDraftGroups`). Each window's groups, in its order, are a list of chat ids and draft groups under `tab-groups.v1` in its own `window` kept file, and which group it shows is its app tab there. The keys main's build reads keep the shapes it reads. Nothing new is written to the workspace folder: a new group is a draft, written to `Drafts/` with the same fields as a new chat's once it has words or files, and closing a group writes nothing about its chat.
- **Multi-window.** A window's rows are its own list, matched to the chat list and the drafts; History is every chat and kept draft that no open window lists, which main reports through `window.live.tabGroupsElsewhere`. Choosing a group another window lists asks main to bring that window forward (`window.showInOtherWindow`, which reads the windows' lists as well as what they show) instead of opening it here too. A new window opened on tabs lists their groups; Shift-click on a row moves the group to a new window. Window > Merge All Windows adds every other window's groups to the front window's list, in their order and once each, with their tabs, then closes those windows.
- **Dragging a tab out or to another window.** A group's tab drags the way a window tab does on main, through `tab-carry.ts`: pulled off its row (`useGroupTabCarry`, around `useTabCarry`) it shows a picture of the window at the pointer, and let go off every window it opens a spare window, which takes it into its own group in place of the new tab it came up on and lists that group alone. Held over another window, that window works out where the tab would land (`useGroupTabLanding`) and tells main (`window.placeGroupTabCarry`): among the tabs of the group it shows, at the pointer, where it draws the tab; in the group of the sidebar row under it, which lights; or as a new group in the room the list opens (`GroupTabLanding`'s `at`). While a tab or a whole group is over a sidebar (`heldOverSidebarAtom`), main hides its picture of the window, and the sidebar draws what is held: a tab in its room as the row it would make (`StandingRow`), or at the pointer as a row where it lands nowhere, and a whole group at the pointer as its row (`HeldPicture`). Anywhere else over that window the drag is free. The window the tab lands in makes the move itself (`receiveGroupTab`, through its own `make` and `moveTab`), and a page's guest follows with its history. A window whose one group is a chat not started yet, with this one tab, is main's window with one tab: the window goes with the tab, and closes once the tab lands in another. The only tab of a group whose chat has not started takes its group along, draft included, and the window it left lets go of the group. Over its own window's sidebar, a tab lands the same ways. Tab > Move Tab to New Window goes the way a tab let go in the open does.
- **Dragging rows and the sidebar's rooms.** One measure says where anything held over a sidebar lands (`sidebarDropAt` in `sidebar-drop.ts`): for a whole group, the room between two rows nearest it, as the rows are laid out, among the pinned rows or the rest; for a tab, the middle of the row under it, or a room: in front of the row whose edge it is near (`ROOM_BAND_PX`), among the pinned rows or the rest, under New, or at the rest's end over the empty list (`data-drop-empty`). A group made in a room among the pinned rows is pinned (`make`'s `place`). The list draws the room by moving the rows past it aside by the carried row's height (`sidebarDropAtom`, whose `top` says where the room starts), a tab's row standing in it and a whole group's room left empty, and a room something lands in shuts at once, the row standing where the room was (`fillRoom`). A row pressed and moved lifts out of the list (`useGroupRowDrag`), and let go lands at its place in the window's list (`listedWith`'s place), pinned or unpinned (`pin`: a chat's star, or a draft's own pin). Pulled well past the sidebar, the whole group goes through `tab-carry.ts` as a group's tab does, under its own key (`wholeGroupKey`) with every page tab's page, and the window it lands in lists it where it was let go and shows it. A window's only row is carried as a window's only tab (`isOnlyTab`): the window goes with the pointer, main closes it once the group lands in another window, and let go in the open it stays where it was let go.
- **Dragging files in.** `useFileDrops` (`file-drops.ts`) listens on the window ahead of everything in it and works out where files held at the pointer land (`FileLanding`): the strip of the group up (`data-group-tabs`), at the place `stripIndexAt` gives; a new tab up (`data-drop-new-tab`); or the sidebar, through `sidebarDropAt` as for a tab. It takes the drag only there, so anywhere else it goes on to the composer's drop region or the window's last resort (`useWindowDropFallback`), which opens every file as one new group. A page sees only how many files a drag carries until it is let go, so main reads their paths off the system's drag (`files.dragged`, the Mac bridge's `dragFiles` reading the drag pasteboard); off the Mac only Files' own drag is known by name (`ownFileDragInAir`). Once named, the files are drawn as the tabs they would be, under ids kept for the drag: over the sidebar as a tab is (`heldOverSidebarAtom` with `files`), and in the strip of the group up (`heldFilesAtom`, `stripWithHeldFiles`), where the first stands at the pointer and the tabs past it make room as they do for a held tab. Let go, `useLandFiles` opens them under those ids, so a tab drawn in the strip stays where it stood. A drag that goes quiet for a second is taken to have gone, for a drag that leaves without a word.
- **Closing several.** A row's menu closes the rows as the list shows them, in a project or a search included, through `closeAll` in `tab-groups.ts`, which takes each off the list as `close` does and moves the window to the row whose menu asked when it was showing one of them. `CloseGroupDialog` stacks the rows of every group whose agent is working.
- **Reopen closed tab.** A closed group tab goes on the window's own closed list (`recentlyClosed` in `app-tabs.v2`, as `groupTab`, standing on its group's address), the list main's Shift+Cmd+T reads, so a window closed since comes back first, by close time, and a blank new tab is passed over. Otherwise the newest tab whose group this window still has open comes back at its place in the group, up.
- **Caption buttons off the Mac.** While the window draws them, the frame sets `--caption-room`, and whichever head reaches the window's top right ends in a `CaptionRoom` spacer that wide: the chat card's, the work's tabs while the chat's card is closed, or the tab bar over a chat that is the whole group.
- **What a tab keeps while it is not up.** Only the group shown is drawn, and in it only the tab up, so a screen tab's screen is drawn afresh each time its tab or group comes back up; nothing of it stays mounted. What it shows comes back from its address (a Files tab's folder, layout, selection, search and open file), what it keeps elsewhere from a store by tab id (`keptByTab` in `kept-by-tab.ts`, emptied of a tab once the tab closes, with its router): in Files, whether the folder is shown beside the file it was left beside, and, for every screen, where what scrolls in it stood (`KeptScrolls` around the tab's router in `GroupItem`). The scrolling goes back once the screen is laid out at its width, since a tab drawn as its group comes up stands for a frame in a split still at the last group's widths, and Files fits the folder beside a newly shown file in that same laid-out frame. Pages live in the browser pool and keep their guest whatever is drawn. A chat's composer keeps its words in its draft.

### Where the prototype stands

Every phase in the order of work is built. What is rough or missing:

- **Dragging a row out of its window**, to a window of its own or another window's sidebar, was checked in a dev instance held off screen by having the window report screen points over another window, since input sent to such a window carries nothing past its edge. So a window following its only group, and the pictures at the pointer, were never seen on screen. Dragging within the list, pinning and unpinning by drag, a tab of this window dropped onto a row or between two rows, and files dropped onto a row, between rows, at a pinned row's edge, on New and on the empty list were checked in the app. A tab from another window dropped between rows was never tried. The list does not scroll while a drag nears its top or bottom.
- **Dragging a tab out**: checked in a dev instance held off screen, so the picture at the pointer and a window following its only tab were never seen. A page carried to another window keeps its back history, though not every time: the history goes in alongside the page's reload (`moveTarget` in the browser view manager), as on main. A group's tab with a used page joins the other window and its page reloads, where main's window tab would take over that window's strip to keep the page live.
- **Dragging files in** was checked in a dev instance held off screen with drags made by DevTools, whose files were written to the drag pasteboard first. No real drag from the Finder or from Files was made, so it is unseen whether the system's drag carries them there for every source, and whether a drag leaving the window or put down with Esc is heard leaving (the one-second quiet covers that). The system draws its own file icon at the pointer, with a + badge, beside the row or tab the window draws, and a page cannot hide it. A Mac bridge built before `dragFiles` cannot name what is held, so files land but nothing is drawn for them on the way.
- **Reopen closed tab** works per window, the way a browser's does. A page comes back at its address in a fresh page, so its back and forward history is gone, as on main. Decisions the plan left open, taken from what the product does today:

- A new tab's field takes the caret whenever the tab comes up, and the chat's composer takes it whenever the chat fills the center.
- The chat tab is the chat card's whole head while the card is open, with its X at the card's top right. Closed, the card leaves the chat tab at the right end of the work's tabs (`ClosedChatTab`), where a press opens it again, as Go to Chat (⌃⌘1) does.
- Only the tab bar over a chat that is the whole group has the darker strip, a darker shade of the card in dark mode and a light gray in light mode, with the chat tab lifted off it as a plain rounded tab. The work's tabs and the chat card's head stand on the card's own ground: the darker strip read as a band across each card's head.
- Only the head that reaches the window's top right leaves room for the caption buttons off the Mac.
- A new chat's composer reads "Talk to Instrument", and its line over it is 1.0's "How can I help?". The glint crosses it again for each new chat that fills its group.
- The commands a chat left running show in the chat tab, before its X.
- A chat's group whose tabs were all dragged to another window is chat first when it is next shown.
- Cmd+W's second press counts within three seconds.
- The sidebar resizes as the inbox did: its right edge drags it between 320 and 440 px, leaving the group at least 560, a drag under 240 slides it shut, and the toggle slides it in and out. It starts at 320 and keeps the width it is dragged to.
- A row dragged out of the window shows its group as it leaves, for the picture at the pointer, and the window goes back to the group it showed before. A row's room is found by the row's top edge, a tab's landing by the pointer.
- A tab dropped as a new group in a project's list is not filed under the project.
- A dragged tab stays the one up, and the window follows it to its group, as a browser's tab dragged to another window does. A group whose chat has not started and that a drag leaves empty is thrown away.
- A tab held over a sidebar where it lands nowhere is drawn hanging just under the pointer; a whole group from another window is drawn centered on it. History takes nothing dropped on it, and the empty list takes a tab only where the list has room under its rows.
- A row a tab would join and the row it would make in a room share one tone, a quiet fill with a hairline, which stands apart from the row up's card.
- A room for a tab or files opens a few px from the line between two rows (8 into each row), and a pinned row's middle joins it like any other.
- The empty list makes a group at the end of the unpinned rows, where it was let go; New makes one at their top.
- Keep open in the dialog for several groups keeps every one of them open.
- Shift-click on a row moves the group to a new window; shift-click on a link opens a new group, as the plan says.
- History is ordered by a chat's last message or reply, and a draft's last change.
- A chat open in another window shows in search with no close in its menu, since closing it is that window's to do.
- Files let go on a row join that group behind what the window shows, so a group can collect files without leaving the one up; in a room, the window comes up on their new group. Several files standing in a room are drawn as the row they make, the first one's title over every one's mark; held where they land nowhere, as the first one's row ending "and 2 more". Let go over the sidebar where a tab lands nowhere (its search, a project's banner, History), files open as a group of their own, as anywhere else nothing takes them.
- A group's name and a chatless group's pin are kept per device in the `view` kept file, not in the draft's `DRAFT.md` or the chat's records: a group of only tabs never writes its draft, and the prototype writes nothing new to the workspace folder.
- A group keeps its typed name when its chat starts, and the chat is named as any chat is.
- A group's copy takes its pin as well as its name, so a pinned group's copy sits right below it among the pinned rows. It does not take a chatless group's typed words.
- A named or pinned group whose chat has not started stays when a dragged-out last tab empties it, and an empty one that is named or pinned is kept when closed.
- The sidebar's search finds a chat's group by its typed name as well.
- A tab asked for from inside a tab (a middle click, a Cmd-click, Open in New Tab, in Files or on a page of a group whose chat has not started) lands right after the tab up, and waits behind it when the gesture asks to, as a browser's link in a new tab does. A folder still opens in place.
