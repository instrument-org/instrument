# The inbox: the 2.0 chat pane shaped like mail, with each thread's tabs

Status: built, fifth version. A thread stands beside its tabs the way a task's page keeps its pane, headed like one; a draft takes the same column with the new-task page's prompt box; the inbox column collapses; the agent's opens land with the thread that asked; the threads stay mounted; archiving and starring work with undo; the inbox rows carry their actions. The dogfooding round after the first beta reshaped the pane's side (topic tiles first, places without counts, All in place of Archive, the head over the list when narrow), put the tasks a thread is working on in a line over its composer, gave the thread menu Rename from the conversation and Save transcript, and settled the bubbles' colors. The second round made a thread's tasks the pane's face rather than tabs and dropped the window-wide tasks screen, said when Instrument is working in the thread and on its row, bound the inbox's chords, and put a topic's details and its way out on the banner. The third round replaced the pane's sections column with a rail down the window's edge naming the app's places, and slimmed the filters to one line over the list with a topic picker at its end. Still to come from the sections below: the groups and drafts kept in the workspace rather than on one computer, and the word on the draft's action.

## The rule

Mail is the metaphor and the furniture is ours. A thread is a conversation the user started; the inbox lists them by when anything last happened, newest at the top, so a reply landing lifts its thread. What the agent opened or made while working in a thread stays with that thread: its tabs, its files, its sites, its tasks. Nothing lives at the window level except the rail (the way in, New, and the app's places) and the inbox column's toggle. The words on screen are ours: Inbox, Needs you, Starred, Drafts, All; never Compose, Label, Attachment, Mail.

## The pane

The rail down the window's left edge, outside the pane: New at its top in the brand's green, round, with the word under it; then the app's places, Home, Chat, and Files, each a mark with its word under it and the one stood in on a lifted tile; and at its foot the user's picture (a gear while signed out) with Settings under it, the way in to Settings. Home and Files are laid out and not yet built: each is an empty area saying so, and the chat's columns wait behind it, hidden rather than unmounted. The place is kept on this computer, and the window opens on the chat. Chat is the inbox. Over its list, one line says where the list stands: the places as marks, Inbox (every thread not put away), Needs you, Starred, Drafts, and All (every thread, put away or not), the one stood in wearing its name and the inbox its count, and at the line's end the topic picker, the word Topic with a caret while no topic is chosen and the chosen topic's mark and name on its tint once one is, opening every topic with a check on the one on and a new one at the foot (the same list a row's tag control opens; a pick closes it, and a topic made from the foot is the one the list then stands in). The one figure is the unread count, on Inbox and on Starred while there are any, the way mail counts its unread on the inbox rather than keeping a place for them. The places are one radio group and the topic another: a topic narrows whatever place the line stands in, so the unread filed under one topic is a click on each, and choosing the same place again steps out of it. There is no app filter; the search finds a thread by its app. The search sits under the line, the way mail puts it, as a pill with its glass and its word centered while it rests empty, and narrows whatever the line has chosen.

The rows take one of two shapes from the list's own width, measured rather than told. Past six hundred pixels a row is one line, mail style: the state dot in front of the title (brand and breathing while the thread works, brand and still while it holds replies not yet seen, amber while it waits on the user, nothing while quiet), the title in a fixed column (semibold while something is unseen), the agent's latest line in muted (the running step in brand, or Instrument is working until a task names one; a question behind an amber glyph with gray words), the holds as marks (files as chips with their names on the card's white, apps as bare marks, sites as their icons and left out when a site has none), the topic pills by name, and the star at the end. Under that width a row is three lines: the dot, the title, and the pills at the line's end in the corner; the latest line clamped to two; the holds on one line, files first, clipped at the edge, with the star at the line's end in the bottom corner. No time and no reply count on either. The typed ask is not on the row; the title stands for it until the agent names the thread. The hairlines between rows stop short of the list's edges and the resting rows keep square corners; the row whose thread is open is a card lifted off the list: the card's ground, rounded corners, an edge. While the pointer is on a row its controls stand in the top corner where the pills step aside (the control that files it first, then its actions); on a slim row they take the pills' place in the line.

With one topic chosen, a banner stands above the rows: the topic's mark and name, its line about itself or how much is filed there since when, a strip of what its threads hold clipped at the banner's edge, and at its right the topic's menu (its details) and an X that takes the topic off the filter, since the banner is the one place that says the list is narrowed.

## The draft

New in the rail, or Cmd+N, starts a draft, which takes the conversation column: a head naming it, with the pane toggle and the way out, and under that the prompt box the new-task page starts a task from, its rounding, plus menu, model picker and arrow. A draft opened inside a topic names that topic in its head as the pill the thread will wear, with an X that unfiles it, so the filing is seen before it happens. Beside the model sits the output picker: a quiet chip saying Output until a page type is picked, then that type's own pictogram and name; it opens the whole catalog of page types in a popover under itself, four across in the catalog's groups, scrolling down, one picked at a time and cleared by the same tile or by Clear. Nothing is picked to begin with, and the arrow never changes. The pick goes with the first message as its output-format part, which the agent reads as the brief's page type and the record shows as a line under the ask. Its tabs are the pane's, beside it, starting with the new-tab page, so a site, a folder, a file, an app, or an idea opened from there is a real tab of the draft's; a draft never runs out of tabs, since closing its last one puts the new-tab page back. The words ride in the draft's record so the Drafts list can name it and a relaunch keeps them; what else the box was given (files, a folder) is kept in memory while the draft is away and put back when it comes up. Closing keeps a draft with words or something gathered in Drafts and throws an empty one away. The arrow starts the thread with what the box sends, the topic, and what the pane's tab shows as the context; then the group is re-keyed to the thread and the thread's conversation takes the column, so the tabs, guests and all, stay exactly where they were. The arrow has no word on it: the right one for starting a thread is still to be found, and Send is not it.

Drafts are rows in the Drafts place, newest first, opened by a click and thrown away from the row. They live in an atom kept on this computer, as the tabs do.

## A thread and its pane

A thread on screen takes the conversation column, headed the way a task's page heads its chat: the title, then the topic pills after it, then the thread's own menu (archive, mark read, star, rename behind a wand, save transcript, topics), and at the right the pane toggle while the pane is closed. The transcript reads as messages, each reply in a bubble at the left on the card's ground lifted by its shadow alone, facing the user's in the brand's light tint at the right; the files a reply hands over stand under its bubble at the column's width as the cards a task page draws; a line the conversation said twice in one turn is drawn once, and a line said again with its files fence after it draws only the fence; the tasks the conversation starts draw no card in it. While the thread's own agent composes, the typing dots stand at the transcript's tail; while a task filed from the thread runs and the agent is quiet, the tail carries the agent's breathing dot and the line Instrument is working, in the place the reply will land. Over the reply field, while tasks filed from the thread are at work, one folded line says what the newest is doing or what a task waits on, and opens to a row per task that opens the task in the pane's face. The threads lately shown stay mounted behind the one on screen, so switching back is the transcript as it was.

The tabs belong to threads and sit in a pane beside the conversation, the way a task's page keeps its pane: a card with the tab strip as its first row and, at the strip's end, the pane toggle on the same pixel the head keeps it while the pane is closed, so pressing it again and again never moves the pointer, and past it the thread's Tasks control, which brings the thread's own tasks up as the pane's face over whatever tab is up, never as a tab, and reads pressed while the face is up: the list (headed Tasks, newest first, the title with the time at its right and the task's own line under it), and a task pressed in it opens in the face's place, the row over the pane reading Tasks then the task, with Tasks a press back to the list and back and forward walking the two; a tab picked or anything opened puts the face away, and every address under the tasks (from Recent, from a reply's link, from the line over the composer) lands in the face of the task's own thread; the address row under the strip, its box a pill with the placeholder centered while a new tab rests empty; the edge between conversation and pane dragged to size them, and past its floor closes the pane. Each group's pane state is its own. Whatever opens while the thread is on screen joins its group as a new tab and brings the pane up; an open from the thread's chat names the thread's group, so it lands there whatever the window has up; the agent's opens carry the thread that asked, so a page or path opened for a thread that is not up lands in that thread's group behind. A task's browser comes to the front of its thread's pane as it arrives, so the user sees it work. A page opened on a site the group already has a tab for goes to that tab, in place of the page it had. A group may hold no tabs: closing the last one closes the pane, and the toggle brings it back with a new tab in it. A thread waiting behind with its new-tab page up is told where to go the way the tab on screen is: what the conversation opens for it lands in that tab rather than beside it. Choosing another thread swaps the whole row silently, and coming back lands on the tab the thread last had up; Alt+Cmd+Down and Alt+Cmd+Up walk the inbox from the thread on screen in the order the list shows. A hold on an inbox row opens its thread at the hold, as a tab of the thread's, with the pane up. Cmd+F or Ctrl+F over a page opens the find bar in it. The window's chords answer the raw key ahead of the page, the way the classic window's do, so the composer's editor cannot keep them.

There is no group of the window's own: nothing on screen means the inbox takes the width. The groups are one flat list of tabs, each carrying its group, kept on this computer across launches. Keeping them in the workspace, the way a task's open tabs were kept before, is the next step.

## Rows and places

A row carries its actions in its corner while the pointer is on it (Topics, Archive or Unarchive, Mark as read or unread) and a menu on a right click with the way in, the actions, Star, Rename, Save transcript, and the topics; a thread is never a tab, so no gesture asks for one. Archiving and unarchiving each show a toast with an undo. Starring is a control in view on a wide row and a mark once given on a narrow one. A topic made from a row or the thread's head is filed on that thread as it lands, and filing paints the pills from the click. The places are Inbox, Needs you (a thread waiting on the user, including a task of its stalled on a question), Starred, Drafts, and All; a thread put away is in All alone, the way mail keeps what was archived out of the inbox but in the whole of it, and a starred one is in Starred wherever it is.

## The bar

The window bar keeps one thing of the inbox's. At its left, past the lights, the inbox toggle puts the inbox column away so a thread and its tabs have the window, and brings it back; closing the thread brings it back too, since nothing else would be left on screen. The tasks badge that stood at its right, and the window-wide list of every task it opened, are gone: a task is looked at in its thread's pane, and the badge's road there went through the thread anyway. Activity is gone altogether, the screen with its door, for the reasons in [decisions/2026-09-19-drop-the-activity-screen.md](../../decisions/2026-09-19-drop-the-activity-screen.md).

A thread's reply is what the desktop notifies about, under the thread's title and with what the turn said, opening the thread when clicked; a task the conversation started reports into its thread and raises no notification of its own, and a turn that only acted says nothing.

## Accepted for this version

- The unread count on Inbox and Starred is the one figure; no count on the topics, and no reply count or time on a row.
- The draft's files and folders are the prompt box's own, sent with the first message the way the new-task page sends them.
- A draft is never minimized: it is closed, and comes back from Drafts.
- The thread's tasks are the pane's face and never tabs, and there is no list of every task across the app.
- The transcript's assistant text sits in bubbles, reversing the rows-not-bubbles rule of the Slack-shaped round, because the conversation column is narrow beside the pane.
- The conversation's prompt no longer makes every long answer a page: a page is one of the forms a file takes, chosen by the picker, the user's words, or the work itself.
- The new-tab page has no Ideas section; the Ideas screen stays.
- No microphone on the draft, since the app has no dictation.

## Open

- The tab groups and the drafts kept in the workspace rather than in browser storage on one computer.
- The word on the draft's action.
- Another thread's task is readable from a thread but not steerable; whether a thread should reach across at all is open.
