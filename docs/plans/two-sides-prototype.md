# Two sides prototype

The tab groups prototype rebuilt as a browser of two sides: every tab is a page with an address, a chat included, and a tab group shows one side of tabs or two. It follows the cofounders' 11-13 review of the tab groups prototype and the built-in apps wireframes. The build with one chat per group, its chat pinned to the right, is kept at the tag `tab-groups-one-chat-per-group` for comparison. Where this plan and `tab-groups-prototype.md` disagree, this one wins, and code of the earlier build that fights it goes.

Page contents can stay rough. What has to be right is the layout engine, dragging and dropping, and moving things around.

## The model

- A window lists its **tab groups** down the sidebar, in its own order, as the earlier build does.
- A group has **one or two sides**. A side is an ordered list of tabs with one tab up. One side of the group has the keyboard.
- **Every tab is a page with an address**: a website, a folder or a file in Files, a chat, a new tab (the zero state), or a built-in page (Settings, Memory, Skills, Chats, Activity, Apps). A chat is an ordinary tab: a group holds any number of chats, on either side, in any order, and nothing is pinned anywhere.
- Only two sides, ever. A third thing joins a side's tabs.
- **A side never shrinks to nothing.** The gutter between the sides resizes them and stops at each side's minimum width. Closing a side's last tab leaves the other side as the whole group.
- **Below the minimum width** the group's sides need, the group scrolls sideways instead of squeezing them.
- **Chats and tab groups are apart.** A group is a record of its own, under an id minted once, and a chat is what it always was. A chat's tab is a tab whose address names the chat, so a chat can have any number of tabs, in any number of groups, and nothing reads a chat out of a group's id or a group out of a chat. Opening a chat always opens it: no tab already on it is brought up in its place, and nothing refuses.
- A group's sidebar row shows the tabs that are up, on two lines: a group of one side its tab across the row, a group of two each side's tab in a half of equal width, with a hairline between. Each leads with its tab's mark and title, and its second line says where it is: a chat's latest line or step, a site, a file's folder, the part of a built-in page open, the words typed in a new tab. The whole row is one press, and under the pointer each half has an X, centered on its two lines, that closes its tab. A pin is the group's own, and a group has no name of its own.
- **A chat's state is its own.** Unread, working and waiting belong to the chat, never to a group, so every row and tab showing a chat shows its state, however many there are. A chat's mark wears one dot for an unread reply or a question waiting (`ChatMarkIcon`) the same way in a strip, a sidebar row and the Chats list, except where the chat is on screen (in a strip, the tab up on its side). A chat's tab in a strip also shimmers its name and mark while the chat works, the tab up included, as a task's tab and a page an agent is using do (`pane-tabs.tsx`); a question waiting outranks work, so a chat asking wears its dot and no shimmer. A failed or stopped chat's tab looks as an idle one's, and its sidebar row says what happened. While Instrument can't be reached, a cloud-with-a-slash icon stands left of History in the sidebar's top row, its card saying what stops working.
- **Closing a group never stops its chats.** A chat at work keeps working with no tab open, and its row is on the Chats page.
- **The sidebar's Chats list**, over the account at the sidebar's foot, set apart from the groups by the foot's one line, under a Chats heading with a chevron that opens the Chats page, shows the chats that came to want a look while no sidebar row showed them (`activeChats`). A row shows a chat when it is the tab up on a side of a group the window lists, the group up included (`chatsInView`); a chat behind another tab is out of view. A chat joins when its reply lands unseen or it asks (a question with `choose` or a card) out of view, and, on the window's first look, when it already wants a look out of view. It leaves once it wants a look no longer: seen, with nothing waiting. A chat that already wanted a look when it went out of view never joins, so switching tabs never changes the list, and work still going is no reason to join. Rows already showing never move: the list grows upward from the foot, so a chat that joins goes on top while fewer than three show and behind the More row once three do, and when a shown row leaves, the next one behind More takes its place. Its rows are the Chats page's own at one line with no controls, led by the chat's mark, archived chats included, and More opens the whole list above it. Picking one brings up the tab already holding it (`tabHoldingChat`: its group up, the tab up on its side, that side with the keyboard), and only when no tab holds it opens it as a new tab right after the tab up on the side with the keyboard. The bell keeps the app's own notices and problems, never chats.
- **The sidebar** is tab groups and projects, with no search of its own. Chats are found from the Chats page, which ⇧⌘F opens with the caret in its search, from the chat list a chat's tab opens beside it, and from a chat's address field.
- **The Chats page** is the Chat screen's inbox from the earlier app, its list and rows the same: every chat by when the person last messaged it, newest at the top, the projects above them, the search, and the Inbox, Drafts and Archived places. At its root the list fills the tab; a chat or a draft picked from it opens to the list's right in that same tab, and Back comes back. Closed recently is a tabs concept alone (⇧⌘T, the + menu, Activity): no list of chats is kept by when their tabs closed.
- **A chat's tab** has the inbox's toggle ahead of the chat's title, which opens the same list beside the chat to switch chats within the tab. It is closed by default, stays closed on a new tab and when its first message is sent, and opens only when the person opens it, or when the chat was opened from the list.

## Opening things

- A click on any page but a chat opens in place, where Back returns, or as a new tab right after the tab it came from, in the same side, ⌘-click leaving it behind. Beyond what opens beside a chat (below), nothing opens in the other side unless the person asks for it: Open in Split, Open Link in Split, Move to Other Side, or a drag. Nothing already open hops to the other side by itself, and no card moves when something opens.
- **What the person opens from a chat follows one rule** (`openBesideChat`, `personPlacementOf` in `beside-chat.ts`), for a link, a file, a folder or a chat chip in it, a page or a file card it shows, and the chat's own folder button:
  - **The one exception, beside:** a plain click in a chat on the left side, or the only side, opens on the right side, made where there is none, in front, with the keyboard. It takes the place of an unwritten New Tab that is all the right side has, else goes right after the tab up there. A tab on the right already showing the same chat, file or folder, or a page at exactly the same address, comes up instead of a second copy.
  - **Everything else is a browser's:** a plain click in a chat on the right, Open Link in New Tab and a Shift-click open a new tab right after the chat in its own side, in front, with the keyboard; a ⌘-click (Ctrl-click off the Mac) and a middle click open one there waiting behind, the keyboard staying put. These always open a new tab, as browsers do.
  - Open Link in Split opens in the other side from the chat (the left for a chat on the right); Open Link in New Tab Group is the only open that makes a group, and Open Link in New Window makes a window.
  - Branching a chat opens the branch as Open in New Tab does: in front, with the keyboard.
- **What a chat's agent opens** keeps its own rule (`agentPlacementOf`): a page it opens (`agent-browser tab new`, a page it starts in) and what it shows (`instrument window show`) go against the chat's tab the person last sent a message from, never in its place and never to its left. Chat on the left, or alone, with nothing beside it: the right side, in front (an unwritten New Tab there is replaced). A right side in use: its last tab, in front only when shown or over the agent's own page, and never while the keyboard is there, so a tab the person has up stays up. Chat on the right: right after the chat, up only when shown. Its later pages go right after its previous one, in that side. It never moves the keyboard.
- Which page types open a plain click beside them is one switch keyed by page type (`OPENS_BESIDE`), only a chat for now.
- **Foreground by default.** Every new tab the person opens comes up with the keyboard unless the gesture asks for the background (⌘-click, middle click). Every menu's Open in New Tab, a link's, a file's, a Files row's, a bookmark's and a chat row's, opens in front; the click gestures reach the same row's background twin (`OpenDestination.runBehind`).
- **The keyboard follows the person, never an agent.** Anything the person opens or drops into a side gives that side the keyboard, whether it made a new side, replaced a tab or joined one: an open from a chat in front (`openBesideChat`, for an open no agent made), a drop the window's drag lands (its moves select what landed), and a drop a side's own page takes, as a folder in Files or a composer (`SideCard`'s drop, as its press). ⌘-click and the like stay behind and move nothing. What an agent opens or shows (`openedBy`) never moves the keyboard.
- **An agent never makes a tab group.** What it opens goes only into the group of its chat's tab, by the rules above: `window show`, `agent-browser tab new`, a page it starts in once it goes somewhere, a file or folder it shows, and a popup or `window.open` from its page, which opens right after that page. A chat with no tab, as one still at work after its group closed, opens nothing: `window show` and `tab new` say the user closed the chat's tab, a page it starts in keeps working with no tab, and a popup from such a page opens nothing (`agentChatTabOf`, `pageOpenPlaceOf` in `agent-opens.ts`). Its asks go to the window that has its group up, else one that lists it (`windowListingGroup`). An agent's page stays after the turn ends, until someone closes it. A blank page the agent starts in or asks for gets its tab only once it goes somewhere, so no blank tab shows in the strip. Every tab in a chat's group is part of its conversation, with nothing to attach: when the person asks about or for a change to what is there, the agent reads it and acts in it directly by its id, a page through `agent-browser` and a file at its path. It goes to another address in a tab the person opened only when asked, and every other page it opens gets a tab of its own.
- **Inside a window, only a menu's Open in New Tab Group makes a group** (and Open in New Window, a window's first). A chat opened from inside the window is a tab like anything else (`chatPlacementOf` in `placement.ts`): clicked in a chat, by the rule above; from anywhere else (a link on a page, the Chats list's ⌘-click, ⌘K, the omnibar, a task whose chat has no tab), right after the tab up on the side with the keyboard, behind on a ⌘-click. ⌘-click, middle click, Shift-click and Open in New Tab open a new tab in the same group, right after the tab they came from (`inGroup` and `gestureOf` in `placement.ts`). A web page's own Shift-click, popup and `target=_blank` open right after the page. Only a window with no group up opens one as its group. ⌘N, New, reopening a closed group, a drag to a room in the sidebar, and what comes from outside (below) are the other ways a group is made.
- **+ and ⌘T** always open a new tab (the zero state) after the tab up, in the side with the keyboard.
- **⌘N** always makes a new group: one side, one new chat, at the top of the list under New.
- **⇧⌘T** reopens what was closed last: a group closed whole comes back as that group, and a tab comes back where it was.
- **⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→** (Alt+Shift with the arrows off the Mac) move the keyboard to the left or right side, as code editors move between splits.
- **⌥⌘N** (Alt+Shift+N off the Mac) opens a new tab on the other side, after the tab up there, with the keyboard, making the right side where the group has one side, as the tab menu's New Tab in Split does.
- **Every chord meaning the tab up acts on the side with the keyboard**, whatever holds the caret: close, back and forward (the mouse's thumb buttons too, pressed over a side, which takes the keyboard first), the address field, reload, find, zoom, print, Bookmark This Tab. A side with no page has no page to reload, zoom or print, and the other side's page is left alone.
- **⌘1 to ⌘8** pick the group's tabs left to right across both sides, the left side's strip and then the right's, and ⌘9 the last; ⌃Tab steps the same order.
- **⌘K** is the launcher: a screen or a site picked there opens in the tab up on the side with the keyboard, where Back returns; a chat opens as a new tab right after that tab.
- **A chat asked for from outside** (a notification, the bell, an `instrument://chat` link from another app) comes up at its tab the person last sent from, wherever it is open; a chat with no tab opens as a group of its own.
- **Files, folders and pages from outside** (an `instrument://file` or `files` link, the website's `/open` hand-off, the Finder, the Dock, a launch's arguments) open as one new tab group in the window in front, a tab each in the order given on one side, the first up (`outsideOpensOf`); a link never places tabs on the other side.
- **Every tab's menu can open a chat beside it** (New Tab in Split), so a group of only websites is one click from the AI.

## The new tab, which is a new chat

A new tab is the chat zero state inside a browser's chrome: the toolbar with an empty address field, the bookmarks bar with Files first, and today's new-chat composer at the middle under them. Its tab, its row in the sidebar and the tab menu's New Tab in Split all call it New Tab. Typing an address in the field and pressing Enter goes there in this tab, and Back comes back to the new tab. Sending a message makes this tab the chat, in place. A file let go on the page opens in this tab, as a browser's new tab does, except over the composer, which attaches it to the first message; once something is written, the tab takes files as a chat's does. Each new tab is its own draft; one that is never typed in leaves nothing behind.

## A side's chrome

Each side is a card of the window's, with its own head:

1. its row of tabs, ending in +;
2. its toolbar: back, forward, reload, then the address field, ending in the star;
3. the bookmarks bar, Files first, with the chevron beside Files that the earlier build added;
4. what the tab up shows.

A chat tab has the same chrome as any tab. Its address field reads as the Chat chip and its title; Back from a chat's first visit does nothing, and a page opened in place from a chat goes back to it. Under the bookmarks bar the chat's title leads its page, where its menu and Rename are, with the chat folder's button at the right, which opens the folder beside the chat. A task's tab has the same chrome, its address field reading as the chat's title, which goes back to the chat, then the task's.

Files keeps the same row as every tab, and its own controls (the sidebar, the view, the sort, search) sit in a second row under the bookmarks bar.

With two sides, the side with the keyboard is drawn with a 2px ink line round its card in the theme's ring color, softened to three quarters (`FOCUSED_SIDE` in `group-view.tsx`), the same ink a place that opens what is let go there is edged with, and the other side's half of the group's sidebar row is the narrower one.

**The address field** leads with an app chip for every built-in page and for a chat (Chats, Files, Settings, Memory, Skills, Activity, Apps), then where the tab is, in parts, the way a file's path reads: a chat's title, a part of Settings, what History shows. The chip goes to the app's index: Chats to every chat, Files to the home folder, and each built-in page to its start. Every part before the last is a place to step back out to, as a file's folders are. Both go in place, from a chat's tab too: only what a chat's own contents open goes beside it. A website shows its URL as today. Clicked, the field holds a website's URL or a file's or a folder's path to edit; the app's own places have no address a person types, so their field opens empty and takes what any field does: a search, an address or a path. Escape puts the place back. Only a click, ⌘L or a letter typed opens the field for typing: a caret the keyboard brings there leaves the place on show. Addresses for bookmarks and links use the link forms the app already opens (`instrument://chat/<id>`, `instrument://file/<path>`, `instrument://screen/<name>`), extended for the built-in pages, and the field never shows one to edit.

**The star** at the end of every address keeps the tab as a bookmark on the bookmarks bar, a chat's and a folder's included, and ⌘D does the same. Its card renames the bookmark or removes it.

**Bookmarks live on the bar.** Other browsers keep pinned tabs in their sidebar, one per row, and this sidebar is a tab group per row. A bookmark there would open in the tab up rather than as a row of its own, which mixes the two, so the sidebar keeps something by pinning its group.

## Built-in pages

Settings, Memory, Skills, Chats, Activity and Apps are tabs with addresses, opened from the address field by name, from the bookmarks bar, from the sidebar (Activity beside the bell at its top, Settings at its foot) and from links. Each must work at half width: a built-in page narrower than 640px folds its own sidebar to a rail of icons, as Files folds its places. Each part of Settings has its own address. Memory and Skills leave Settings for pages of their own. ⌘Y opens Activity, which lists each tab and tab group the window closed among its other rows by when it closed, under its Tabs filter too (`useClosedTabs`, from the list ⇧⌘T reopens): a row's details name the tabs it had, and Restore brings it back as ⇧⌘T would. Settings open beside a chat while the agent changes a setting is the case to get right.

## Menus are the main way to make and manage splits

For now every split is made and managed from right-click menus, the same items wherever they apply:

- **A tab**: Open in Split (a copy on the other side) or Move to Other Side, New Tab in Split, Move to New Tab Group, Move to New Window, Duplicate, Bookmark, Close, Close Other Tabs, Close Tabs to the Left, Close Tabs to the Right. A chat's tab then has the chat's own items, each acting on that chat: its marks, Rename, Project, Copy (the chat folder's path, the work folder's path, for an agent), Send Feedback, Archive and Delete. A new chat's tab with words in it has Project, which files its draft.
- **A link** (in a page, in a chat, in a file shown in a tab): Open Link in Split, Open Link in New Tab, Open Link in New Tab Group, Open Link in New Window, Bookmark Link, Copy Link. A page's menu, and a file's, start with Open Link, which opens the link in place; a chat's has no plain Open, since a click there does it. A file or folder in Files, or a file chip in a chat, keeps a file's words (Open in Split, Add to Bookmarks).
- **A sidebar row**: Open in New Window, Duplicate, Pin, the Close items. Each acts on the group as a whole, whatever its tabs show; what acts on one chat is in that chat's tab menu.
- **A bookmark**: Open, Open in Split, Open in New Tab, Open in New Tab Group, Open in New Window, Rename, Delete.

Moving a tab to the other side and joining the two sides into one (Join Sides, the left side's tabs and then the right's) are in the tab menu only, with no keys. On-screen controls for splitting come later.

## Dragging and dropping, through one system

One drag system carries everything that can be held, and every place that takes a drop answers through it, so hover states, drop marks and what rides on the cursor agree everywhere.

- **Held**: a tab, a group (its sidebar row), files (from Files or from the Finder), and a link: anything else the app names by an address, a site's link in a page or a reply, a chip in a reply or the composer (a chat, a task, a file, a page), a row of the Chats list, the address field of a tab while it is not being edited (its chip and parts included, the place it shows held as its address, as a browser's address bar drags it), and a bookmark, which is a link the bar keeps.
- **Landings**:
  - a side's tab row, between two tabs;
  - a side's page: with one side, the left or right half lights and letting go makes that half a side holding the tab; with two, the side under the pointer lights and takes it as its last tab;
  - a sidebar row (joins that group) or the room between two rows (a new group there);
  - the bookmarks bar, between two bookmarks (keeps it as a bookmark there);
  - a web page, for files only: the page gets the files as if dropped from the Finder;
  - another window, and the open desktop (a new window); a link let go past the window's edge opens in a window of its own.
- A link or a chip opens as the tab its address names wherever it lands, as a tab, a side, a new tab turning into it, a group of its own in a room or a tab of a row's group.
- **Moves**: tabs within a side, between sides, between groups and between windows; rows within the list and between windows; bookmarks along the bar, off it (removed, with Undo in a toast) and back on; files onto tabs, rows, sides and web pages.
- **What shows on the way says what letting go does**, as soon as the pointer is there, with no wait for it to rest: tab-shaped over a tab row, with the others making room, or one line where it would stand; the row it joins lit; a row standing in a room; the side it would take lit, or, at a page's outer edge, a line down that edge and the half a split takes lit, nothing in the group drawn again until letting go; a bookmark standing on the bar; a whole group pulled free of the sidebar, a picture of the window letting go makes; off every window only, a picture of the tab's page, as the tab alone in a window of its own (a whole group's, of its window). Nothing shows at a drag's start: a split is armed only at a page's outer edge (and the margin past it), never from the middle of a page or a tab row's empty end, 96px deep for a tab, a bookmark or a whole group, which no page's middle takes, and 24px for files and links from the system's drag, so a drag inside a page (a row of Files carried to a folder) meets no split short of the edge. Where letting go would change nothing, nothing is lit, the pointer is the not-allowed cursor, and letting go does nothing. The earlier build's rules for rows, rooms and files stand, and the rest are made to match them. What is held stays at the pointer the whole drag, raised at full strength with a line under it saying what letting go does, over every landing, except over a tab row, where the tab itself stands at the pointer.

## A chat knows the tabs around it

- The chat's agent works in the group of the chat's tab the person last sent a message from. It is told that group once, as a terse block: each side's tabs in order by id, which each side shows, and itself as `you`, and that none of it is on screen while the window shows another group (`isShown`). After that it is told only what changed: tabs opened, closed, moved within or between sides or groups, navigated, now showing on their side, the group going off or back on screen, and this chat's own moves; a message after which nothing changed carries no note. Moving to another group tells it the new group whole. How the notes read is said once in the system prompt. `window show` and `window list` say a tab's place in its own words (`tabPlace`, `showingLine`), never "in front".
- The group's pages go in a second note with what each costs (asleep, out of sight, memory to 100 MB, CPU from 10%), told whole once and then as the pages that changed or went (`browserStatusChangesModelNote`).
- None of this is drawn for the person, who made or watched every change in the window: the notes are developer's notes in the transcript.
- Reach is generous: the agent acts on any tab by its id, and lists any group by its id, by default the group it works in.
- Being in the group is the grant. A file open in the chat's group opens to the chat with each message, as one sent with it does; a folder there is only named: the chat reaches it once the person attaches it with + or drops it on the prompt. Beside the plus in a chat's composer, and a new tab's the same way, a quiet count of the group's other tabs, every one a tab whether it shows a page, a file or a folder, reads "3 tabs" ("1 tab" for one), only while there are some, so it never makes the box taller; hovering it says each message shares them and lists each by its mark and title. No tab of the group is a chip in the composer, and neither is another chat: a chip there is only something the person attached, and a file dropped in is that file alone.

### How it's built

- **Parts in the chat's own log.** `data-groupLayout` (`LayoutSnapshotSchema`, `schemas/group-layout.ts`) is a diff part: written on the chat's own session only when its shape (`sameLayout`: group, tabs by id, address and kind, which shows, whether the group is on screen) differs from the last layout the log has, so a title changing, the keyboard moving or the chat's own tab turning from a new chat into the chat writes nothing, and never for a first layout that is the chat alone. It keeps the layout alone, never where the other tabs on the computer are. `data-layoutChanges` goes beside it when there is a layout before it, and the two are said as one note (`tabGroupModelNote`): the changes when there are some, the whole layout when there are none to say or the chat moved groups. `layoutParts` (`lib/group-layout-parts.ts`) decides both from the chat's records, so a part once written is never rewritten. They go on a person's message (`newMessage`) and on a wake that starts a turn (`deliver` in `wake-queue.ts`), never mid-turn.
- **The diff is pure.** `layoutChanges(before, after, moves?)` in `lib/group-layout.ts` names closed and moved-out tabs first, then each tab of the new layout in order: opened or moved in, moved between sides, moved within its side (tabs outside the longest run that kept its order), now shown on its side, navigated; and last the group going off or back on screen. A different group id is the chat moving groups, said alone. Groups are named by id. When one side becomes two, the side sharing more tabs goes on, the left on a tie; when two become one, the side that gave more tabs. An opened or closed tab an agent opened is marked `byAgent`, read from the tab's `openedBy`. `layoutChangesLine` is the developer's note's line ("Opened Zendesk pricing beside this chat").
- **The window reads it.** `groupReadOf` (`group-awareness.ts`) reads a group through Core's `groupLayoutOf`: the chat's tab is this chat, the keyboard is the side that has it, and every tab is named, a new chat as New Tab. With it go the tabs that moved between the group and another (`movedIn`, `movedOut`), each by the other group, read from the group a tab last moved from (`movedFrom`, set by `moveTabTo`); that is all the moved in and moved out lines need. A send carries the read of the sending tab's group as `layout` on `message.create` (`layoutReadOf`, `shell.layoutContext(tabId)`), which also marks that tab as the one sent from (`promptedAt`). A wake that starts a turn asks the chat's window for it with the `window.tab` list ask (`layoutNow` in `wake-queue.ts`), waiting up to `WAKE_LAYOUT_TIMEOUT_MS` (1s); with no window in the app it asks nothing, and with no answer it writes no layout, so the log keeps the last it had. Nothing keeps a read in the workspace. `window list` answers with the same read, one line per tab with its id.
- **The transcript** draws neither part; both are developer's notes. A message's view context names only what the person pointed at (the page beside the chat, a file, what they picked), never the group's tabs, which are the layout's.
- **Checked by** `group-layout.test.ts` (the diff, `it.each` over a line notation), `group-layout-parts.test.ts` (when parts are written), `wake-layout.test.ts` (a wake asking the window, and its timeout), `group-layout-model-text.test.ts` (the notes), `group-awareness.test.tsx` (the reading), the model-text byte snapshots, and the eval `group-layout-beside` (`layout` on a case or follow-up sends the read with no window behind it).

## Tracks

The build is split into tracks that land on the prototype branch one at a time:

1. **Core**: the model (groups of one or two sides, every tab a page, a chat as an ordinary tab, any number of chats), the layout engine (sides, gutter, minimum widths, scrolling), the new chat as the new tab, opening rules, removing the pinned chat column and the earlier build's chat-first layout, and the tab menu's split items.
2. **Address**: app chips, a page's or a file's real address on the caret, the field's search in the app its chip names, the star and its menu, ⌘D, bookmarks of any address.
3. **Pages**: Settings as a tab with addressable parts, Memory, Skills, Chats, Activity and Apps as pages, folding at half width.
4. **Awareness**: the layout and its changes in each message, read by the agent, shown minimally.
5. **Drag**: one drag system for everything above.
6. **Menus**: the menus above everywhere they apply.

## How it's built

### Core

Paths are under `apps/studio/src/client/components/window/` unless named.

- **The model** (`tab-model.ts`): `WindowTabs` is one list of tabs across the window's groups, each naming its `group`, with `activeByGroup` (the tab up on the side with the keyboard) and `besideByGroup` (the tab up on the other side). A tab on the right side says `side: 1`, and `normalizeSides` turns a group with every tab on one side back into one side. `groupLayoutOf(state, group)` reads a group's sides, each its tabs in strip order and the one up, and which side has the keyboard; `useWindowTabs().layoutOf` is the same from React. `besideOf`, `sideIndexOf` and `chatUpIn` read the rest. The tab a chat's agent works from is `workingTabOf` (`shared/working-tab.ts`), which main reads too: of the chat's tabs in groups an open window lists, the one with the latest `promptedAt`, else the first. A tab carries `openedBy`, the chat whose agent opened it, and on a chat's tab `promptedAt`, when the person last sent the chat a message from it. `groups` holds what each group keeps of its own: its pin and the left side's share of two.
- **Group ids are opaque.** A new group is `group:<uuid>`, and nothing reads a chat or a draft out of one. A group's address is `/chats?group=<id>`. `/chats/<id>` and `/chats?draft=<id>` asked of the window open that chat or draft as a group of its own: `openChat` and `openDraft` (`tab-groups.ts`).
- **Every tab is a page with an address.** A chat is a screen tab at `/chats/<id>` (`GroupChatScreen`, `chat-tab-screens.tsx`). A new tab is a new chat at `/chats` (`GroupNewChatScreen`), at `/chats?draft=<id>` once it has words, and sending walks that tab to the chat in place (`use-drafts.ts`). The composer offers no addresses: what is typed there is the first message.
- **Kept state** is local to this computer: `tab-groups.v3/<group>` in `window/groups.json`, one member per group in the shape the agent reads a group in, `{ sides: [{ tabs, up }], focused, pinned?, share? }`, no tab keeping its group or side; the window holds the flat list in memory and only `splitTabGroups` and `joinTabGroups` (`window-tabs.ts`) read and write the shape. Each window's list is `tab-group-list.v2`, and a group exists only while some window lists it: main removes the rest, and any with no tabs, at launch and when a closed window is forgotten (`unlistedTabGroups`). Nothing an earlier build kept is read, and a kept file that is not JSON is set aside, never read as empty. Upkeep (`upkeptList`, `tab-groups.ts`) gives a pinned group with no tabs a new chat, and lets an unpinned one go. Main reads the chat that opened a tab (`keptOpenerOfTab`) and a chat's working tab (`workingTabOf`) from the same records.
- **One function opens, one moves.** `openInGroup` and `windowTabs.openIn` place a tab by `{ after, group, side }` and `select`; `moveTabTo` and `windowTabs.moveTo` move one between sides, groups or places in a strip. `focusSide` gives a side the keyboard and `raiseTab` brings a tab up on its side. What the person does gives a side the keyboard in one place, its card (`SideCard`): a press anywhere on it, a drop its page takes, or the caret landing in it, caught on the way down, and one of its pages taking the caret, so no kind of tab needs code of its own. The chords read the side with the keyboard from the card that has it (`keyboardSide`, `keyboardPlace` in `lib/keyboard-place.ts`), which names its page (`data-page-tab`, read by `pageForChord`). The caret goes with the keyboard on every path: the side's home takes it (`keyboard-home.ts`; a file's is its editor or its view), nothing on the other side takes it as it comes up, and a caret left there counts as on nothing, its selection dropped so dictation lands nowhere rather than in that side's composer. The openers (`use-openers.ts`) choose `after`: a click opens in place or right after its source on the same side; a chat's link right after the chat's tab, up, or behind on ⌘-click; + and ⌘T a new chat right after the tab up on the keyboard's side; ⌘N a new group; a page an agent opens right after the chat's tab, behind.
- **The layout engine** (`GroupSides`, `group-view.tsx`) draws a card per side, each with its own head: the strip and its + (`pane-tabs.tsx`), then the toolbar on every tab, chats included, and the bookmarks bar (`group-item.tsx`). Two sides split by a share kept per group, dragged at the gutter and snapping to half within `SIDE_SNAP_PX` (15). A side is never narrower than `SIDE_WIDTH_MIN` (240, narrow enough to keep a small page beside a chat; a chat's composer and a page's toolbar crowd at it); below that the group scrolls sideways, and the browser pool clips each guest to the scroller (`data-guest-clip`, `clipInset` in `client/lib/browser-pool.ts`). A page moving sides keeps its guest. A side opening grows its place from its end of the row as the other narrows, and one closing shrinks away as the other widens, on Files' beside curve (`SIDE_MOTION` in `lib/motion.ts`, `SideMotion`; which sides open and close, `useSideMotion` in `side-motion.ts`), the sidebar row's half moving with it; sides keep their keys through a change by the tabs they share (`sideKeysOf`), so the side that stays is never drawn afresh.
- **Closing.** A side's last tab closing leaves the other side as the group, the x or ⌘W on a group's last tab closes the group, with no warning, and the group goes on the recently closed list (⇧⌘T, Activity). Closing the window's last group makes a new one.
- **The composer's line** (`AwarenessLine`, `chat-tab-screens.tsx`, in a chat's tab and a new tab alike) counts the group's other tabs as the agent reads them (`layoutReadOf`), with a hover card listing them. The tab up on the other side always goes with the message as what is in view (`besideOf` into `chatContextItems`, `context-items.ts`, for a new tab's first message too), and is never among the chips over the message (`attached`, `send-context.ts`), nor grants what is selected in it; a chat beside it goes as nothing. The group's files go as `groupFiles`, which `newMessage` grants. A message's list of its group's tabs leaves out chat and new chat tabs.
- **Sidebar rows** (`group-sidebar.tsx`, `row-half.tsx`, `row-lines.ts`): `GroupRow` draws each side's tab up with `RowHalf`, and `useRowLines` says its two lines from the tab, the chat list and the drafts (`RowSources`, which a picture gives its own); a chat at work shimmers its line as the chat list's working line does, while its mark stays as it is. A task's tab reads where it stands from its chat's task list (`useTasksOf`): at work, its row's line shimmers its step and its tab in a strip shimmers as an agent's page does; a failed one says Stopped; it never asks anything, since its chat does. A page's loading and failure come from its guest's load events. Starring a chat does not pin its group.
- **The tab menu** (`PaneTabMenu`, `pane-tabs.tsx`): Open in Split, Move to Other Side, New Tab in Split, Move to New Tab Group, Move to New Window, Duplicate, Close, Close Other Tabs, Close Tabs to the Left and to the Right.

### Address

Paths are under `apps/studio/src/`.

- **Addresses**: `addressOfTab(tab)` (`client/bookmarks/address.ts`) gives any tab's address: a site's URL, a file or folder as `instrument://file/<path>`, and anything else the app shows as `instrumentAddressOf(href)` in `shared/instrument-screens.ts` names it (a chat as `instrument://chat/<id>`, a built-in page as `instrument://screen/<name>`, a part of Settings as `instrument://settings/<part>`). `targetOfAddress` reads one back, and `sameAddress` compares two, so a trailing slash or an escaped character finds the same bookmark.
- **Opening**: `useOpenAddress()` (`client/bookmarks/open-address.ts`) opens any address here, in a new tab behind, on the other side of the group on screen, or as the first tab of a new tab group or a new window, and `openHowOf` reads which from a click (⌘ a new tab, ⇧ a new tab group). A screen a group's tab cannot stand at opens where the app keeps it.
- **The field**: `TabLocationRow` takes the tab's `address` and `title`. The chip is `AppChip` (`client/components/window/app-chip.tsx`), picked by `locationChip` in `tab-location.ts`; a new tab has none. With the caret the field holds `locationText`: a page's URL, a file's `file://` address, a folder's path, and nothing for the app's own places; `address` (`addressOfTab`) is only what the star keeps. Its rows are `OmniList` (`omnibar-list.tsx`). A press on the field and ⌘L open it for typing through `editField` (`omnibar-field.ts`); focus arriving any other way leaves the place on show until a letter is typed. The field opens a typed `instrument://` address on Enter, and offers built-in pages (`builtInPages()` in `shared/instrument-screens.ts`, each part of Settings among them), chats and the app's own bookmarks as rows from two letters of a name, first when the name is typed whole.
- **The star**: `BookmarkStar` (`bookmark-star.tsx`) and its card. ⌘D is the window command `bookmarkTab`, in the Bookmarks menu, and `requestBookmarkTab()` presses the visible star with the most ancestors in common with the focused element, so each side's row answers for its own side. A code editor keeps ⌘D.
- **Bookmarks** are on the bar (`bookmarks-bar.tsx`), which opens them through `bookmarkClicks` and `BookmarkContextMenu` (`bookmark-menu.tsx`), each wearing its tab's mark (`AddressMark`). A stored bookmark that names a place (`where`) reads as one on the bar at its order, since `electron-main/bookmarks/bookmark-store.ts` reads only `order`, `title` and `url`.
- **For other tracks**: `useBookmarkActions()` (`client/bookmarks/actions.ts`) gives `bookmark({ title, url })`, `isBookmarked(url)`, `bookmarkOf(url)`, `rename` and `remove`, and re-exports `addressOfTab`. A tab's or a link's Bookmark item is `bookmark({ title, url: addressOfTab(tab) })`.
- **One mapping**: which built-in page an href is at and its address are `screenOfHref` and `instrumentAddressOf` in `shared/instrument-screens.ts`; `address.ts` keeps only the tab-level layer over them: sites, files and folders.

### Pages

- **Every built-in page is a row of `SCREENS`** (`shared/instrument-screens.ts`): its name, title, address and what it is for, which the product guide lists for the agent. `instrumentAddressOf` gives a tab's `instrument://` address from it: `instrument://screen/<name>` for Activity, Apps, Chats, Files, Memory, Skills and the rest, `instrument://settings/<part>` for a part of Settings. `screenOfHref` reads an address back to its page and part, `builtInPages()` lists every page and part of Settings with its address for the field's rows, and `ScreenMark` (`app-chip.tsx`) is each page's mark, so the tab strip, the chip, a row and a bookmark name a page the same way.
- **Settings is a tab** at `/settings/<part>`, one address per part in `SETTINGS_SECTIONS` (`general`, `account`, `providers`, `permissions`, `storage`, `activity`, `developer`), and `/settings` goes to General. All of Settings is one tab (`tabIdentity`), so asking for it brings the one open forward and walks it to the part asked for (`bringForwardShowing` in `built-in-pages.tsx`). A name that is not a part (`/settings/theme`) is a setting's link, which the route sends to the setting's part with its row lit. What an address asks of the page once (a row to light, a memory to show, the account's usage, the diagnostic log) the page takes and drops from its address (`useArrivingAsk`), so going back never asks it again; the search's words stay in it as `q`.
- **Opening a page.** `openSettings` and `openBuiltInPage` put a request in `pageRequestAtom`, and each window's `BuiltInPageOpener` brings the page forward in the group up, or opens it as a new tab after the tab up. The sidebar's Settings, ⌘,, links into Settings, the command menu's Settings, Memory and Skills rows, the sidebar's Activity button and ⌘Y all go through it. There is no Settings dialog.
- **Memory and Skills** are pages of their own (`/memory`, `/skills`): the sections Settings had, in `BuiltInPage`. Settings' search still finds them and opens them in its tab, and their old links (`instrument://settings/memory`, `memory-import`, `new-skill`) open them.
- **Chats** is `/chat-list` (`ChatsPage`, `chats-page.tsx`), the index the Chats chip goes to (`CHATS_INDEX_HREF`): the earlier app's `ChatPane` and `ChatList`, brought back whole, with the window's `chatFiltersAtom` as their filters; `project` and `q` in the address stand the list in a project or search it. Picking a chat or draft pushes its address in the same tab and marks the tab as showing the list (`useChatListOpen`, by tab id, in memory), so the chat's tab draws it beside the chat (`WithChatList`, a `StudioSidebarRail` 320 to 440px wide, `chat-list-width.v1`). `ChatListToggle` sits ahead of the title in a chat's head and at the same spot on a new tab.
- **The chip and the parts** (`tab-location-row.tsx`): the chip goes to `appIndexHref(app)` (`app-chip.tsx`) through the row's `onVisit`, which walks the tab in place, a chat's included. A built-in page's location carries the `part` shown (a part of Settings, Activity's filter, with `partHref`) and the `item` open in it (Activity's open row, from `useTabSurface("item", …)`), and `locationCrumbs` makes the part a crumb to `partHref` while an item is open. A part of an app page goes through `onVisit` too, since the window keeps one tab per built-in page and would only bring it up as it is.
- **Half width.** Under `PAGE_FOLD_PX` (640), measured on the page itself (`useIsNarrower`), a page folds what it keeps beside its content. Settings' sidebar becomes a rail of the parts' icons, each naming itself on hover, its search opening over the part from the rail's magnifier. Activity's details open under the row picked, and its search and filters go under its title. Memory and Skills are one column whose padding follows a container query. Apps already lays out by container query, and Files folds its places at its own 576px.
- **Live values.** Every part reads its preferences and lists through live queries, so Settings beside a chat shows a setting changed elsewhere as it changes.

### Drag

Paths are under `apps/studio/src/client/components/window/` unless named.

- **One session** (`drag/session.ts`): a window has at most one drag, whatever holds it: the pointer (a tab, a sidebar row, a link), the system's drag (files from the Finder, Files or a file card), or main, for another window's tab or row held over this one. What is held is a `Held` and what letting go would do a `Landing` (`drag/types.ts`); `dragAtom` is what the window draws from, and `dragPoint` is the pointer, read without drawing again. Every press becomes a drag past `START_PX` (8) through `pressToHold`, which takes what is held from where the press was in it (`pressLink` for every link), and Escape, the window losing focus and a canceled pointer all end it in one place. A page runs in a process of its own that takes every move over it, and the release, from a press in the window, so pages let the pointer pass from the press on (`data-pressing`, then `data-tab-held` for the drag, `globals.css`): without it a bookmark pulled straight down onto the page under the bar never moved far enough in the window to become a drag. A move with the button up ends a press whose release the window never heard. A page with the keyboard hears the window's keys and its loss of focus in a process of its own (on a Mac the window's `document.hasFocus()` reads false meanwhile), so the press and the drag hold pages off the keyboard (`holdKeyboardFromPages` in `page-keyboard.ts`), and the page a press brought up takes it once the press ends as a click or the drag ends. A page that takes it anyway blurs the window with the page as `document.activeElement`, which ends nothing: the drag takes the keyboard back and puts it home after. A release's click is swallowed.
- **Every place that takes a drop registers a target** (`useDropTarget`): where it takes drops, what letting go at a point does (a landing, `"native"` where only the system's drag can hand files over, or `"refused"` where letting go changes nothing), a list that scrolls near its ends, and, for a target that does its own landing, `land`, which then takes the drop in place of the lander. The session measures once a frame, the smallest target under the pointer first, so a strip answers before the page around it. The targets are each side's strip (`useStripTarget`, `drag/use-group-drag.ts`), a group's side pages (`useSidePagesTarget`), the sidebar (`useSidebarTarget`, over `sidebar-drop.ts`), the bookmarks bar (`useBookmarksTarget`, `drag/use-bookmarks-drag.ts`), Files' Favorites list (`useFavoritesTarget`, `favorites-drops.ts`) and, for a held place alone, a composer's box (`FileDropRegion`). Where no target answers at all, a Favorites row lands as unfavorite, in the window or past it (`landingOverNothing`, `drag/session.ts`).
- **What letting go does** for every pair of what is held and where it lands is one answer, `outcomeOf` (`drag/letting-go.ts`): move, open, keep, favorite, unfavorite, move into a folder, add to a message, or nothing, and the line said under what is held. The lander acts on it and the drag layer says it, and `letting-go.test.ts` pins the whole table.
- **One lander** (`useLander` in `drag/use-drag-system.ts`) does what letting go does, through Core's moves and openers and the bookmarks' ops: a tab moves; files and a link open as tabs at the same places (`opensOf`: a link by `targetOfAddress`, files as Files opens them), each under the id its stand-in was drawn under, a bookmark staying kept, as Open in Split leaves it; a row takes its place in the list; a tab or a bookmark let go on the bookmarks is kept there (`useKeepBookmark`), and a tab whose address is kept already moves that bookmark there, as an address is kept once; a bookmark let go where nothing takes it is removed, with Undo in a toast (`useRemoveBookmarkWithUndo`).
- **A side's page** (`sideZoneAt`, `drag/landing.ts`): with one side, only its outer edge splits, drawing a line down the edge and lighting the half the new side takes, for anything held but the side's only tab. How deep the edge reaches depends on what is held (`edgeAt`): a tab, a bookmark or a whole group, which no page's middle takes, arms it `PIECE_EDGE_PX` (96px) in from either end, so the deeper edge costs the page nothing; files and links from the system's drag arm it `EDGE_PX` (24px) in, since Files' rows, a composer's box and a site take them everywhere short of that. Once armed, the edge holds farther in, in the same proportion (`EDGE_KEEP_PX`, 40, and `PIECE_EDGE_KEEP_PX`, 160), so a hand resting near it keeps it. Away from the edge content (files, a link) opens as a new tab of the side, and a tab or a whole group lands nowhere. A whole group pulled free of the sidebar is already inside the left edge, since the sidebar keeps it to the list 72px past its own edge (below): an edge a group comes free inside arms nothing until the pointer has been out of it once, then arms as any edge does, each time it comes free (`waitingEnd`, `drag/use-group-drag.ts`). With two sides, content joins the side under the pointer, a tab joins the other side only at its outer edge, and a whole group lands nowhere. Nothing waits for the pointer to rest. The margin between an outer page and the window's edge is that page's edge (`pagesReach`, for a page within 24px of the edge, and on past the edge as far as the pointer is still in the window), so a tab flung to the window's edge splits too. A held tab, group or bookmark lands only in a tab row or a split, never into a page's content, and a held place only in a tab row, arming no split at a page's edge (`pageLandingOf`, `drag/accepts.ts`). Away from the edge a site takes content natively, so does a folder in Files, and a new tab up turns into it; a composer's own box (`FileDropRegion`, a few pixels past the box) takes it wherever it shows short of the edge, with its "Drop to attach to your message" outline, and the rest of the page around it takes it as the page does. A file this app dragged counts as a stray click let go again only over the box it was pressed in (`isSelfFileDrag`), so one carried from another chat or from Files attaches at once. A whole group (its sidebar row) at a split makes a side of all its tabs, in their order, the one up on the half it was pressed by up there, and closes (`tabsOfHeldGroup`, `drag/held-tabs.ts`). A group goes to a window of its own only when let go outside the window.
- **Nothing is drawn in the group before letting go**: while a split is armed, the edge's line and the half it makes are lit, and the group under it is left as it is, with no side mounted, resized or given the keyboard. Letting go makes the split, which then slides in on `SIDE_MOTION`.
- **The sidebar keeps a lifted row** until the pointer goes `SIDEBAR_PULL_PX` (72) past its edge (`isPulledFree`, `drag/use-sidebar-drag.ts`): the row stays in the list's column, giving a little as the pointer pulls, and lands in the list. Pulled free, it rides at the pointer, arming no split at the edge it came free inside until the pointer has left it (`pullOf`), and where letting go makes a window of its own it becomes a picture of that window, a small sidebar beside the group's tab up. Back over the sidebar, it keeps to the list again.
- **What shows on the way** comes from the target the drag is over, never from two places at once. A strip stands what is held among its tabs as tabs, the first at the pointer (`useSideStrip`, drawn by `tab-strip.tsx`); the sidebar lights a row or opens a room (`sidebar-drop.ts`); the bookmarks stand what is held where it would be kept (`useShownBookmarks`), a tab under its address and title or as the bookmark its address is kept as, the bookmark that moves gone from its old place. `drag/drag-layer.tsx` draws the rest, with rings and one line only and no placeholders: a ring round a tab, a folder or a Favorites row it goes into; one line (`DropLine`) in a strip, between Pinned rows, or down a page's edge; a side, the half a split takes, or a new tab lit; and at the pointer the whole drag, raised (`HELD_LOOK`): a chip, a whole group's row, or its window picture, everywhere but a strip, which stands the tab at the pointer itself. Under it, a line says what letting go does (`outcomeOf`): Open on the left, Open on the right, Move to the right, Move 3 tabs here, Move to Reading, Add to Reading, Move here, Open in a new tab, Open in this tab, Add to bookmarks, Open in a new window, Move into Downloads, Copy into Downloads, Add to Favorites, Remove from Favorites. Under the system's own picture of a drag (files, a page's link) the line goes alone. Every place that takes a drop wears one of two looks by what letting go there does (`DROP_LOOK`, `drag/tone.ts`): the brand's dashed green edge only on a composer's box, where letting go attaches to the message (`attach`), and everywhere letting go opens or moves something a solid edge in the theme's ring color over a faint wash (`open`). The open look is near the outline of the side with the keyboard, drawn inside its place and washed so the two never read as one.
- **Places of Files' sidebar and the Files menu drag as places** (`pressPlace`, `drag/press-bookmark.ts`): this app's own chip at the pointer, holding a link to Files at that folder, never the folder itself, so it opens as a tab from a strip or is kept on the bookmarks bar; no page's content takes it and no page's edge splits for it.
