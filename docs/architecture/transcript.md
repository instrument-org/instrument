# Transcript

How a session's records become what a person reads. A chat and a task's tab draw through the same `Transcript` ([transcript.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/transcript/transcript.tsx)); a task differs only in its first turns being its chat's, folded behind one line, and its opener being its assignment.

## The layers

1. **Records.** A session's messages and parts, as [chat-records.md](chat-records.md) describes them.
2. **Light messages.** `transcriptMessage` ([transcript-message.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/transcript-message.ts)) turns each tool call and thought into a `StepPart`: its words, its status, and `shows`, what it can put on a card, cut to a card's length. The command and output stay behind, so a transcript of long scripts costs what its words cost.
3. **The feed.** `message.live.feed` ([feed.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/feed.ts)) sends the session's last turns once, then only what moved: a text part's new characters as an append, a part's change as a patch. `message.older` asks for the page before, and `message.live.whole` reads one message whole for a step someone opened.
4. **Turns.** `turnsOf` ([turns-of.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/turns-of.ts)) decides everything drawn, and no component reads a part to choose what to draw. The same messages always give the same turns, so changing a rule rewrites no records.
5. **Rows and cards.** [`components/transcript/`](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/transcript/) draws what `turnsOf` says. It reaches the app only through `TranscriptSurface` ([surface.ts](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/transcript/surface.ts)): pictures, opening files and tasks, stopping a task, a step's whole part. That seam is what lets the transcript debug page drive the same components from a script, with no records under them.

## What a turn is

Everything since the message the person typed. A wake while the agent is still working stays inside it. One that comes after the agent answered (a task finished, a permission or sign-in answered) opens a turn of its own with no message, below the answer, so the turn that ended keeps its fold and footer and nothing above moves.

A turn's rows are its activities, asks, task cards, notes, errors and words. An activity is a run of calls the agent gave one `activity` label, shown with its explanation while it works and its counts and clock once done. A thought draws no row: one going on is Thinking, which opens onto its words in case the model looks stuck, and one that ended leaves nothing behind. The card asking how a turn went is no row: `ratingCard` on the turn sits under its footer, so it reads as the app asking rather than as part of the reply.

**The fold.** A turn folds all of its work into one Worked row, or none of it, and folds only when nothing in that work is left to read: it finished on its own with words, no message joined it mid-turn, nothing but words comes before its first step, and every row from that step to the reply is a finished step, a card already answered, or words said along the way. A question the person wrote past, typing a message instead of answering, is closed. A note, an error, a task card, a step still running, or a card still waiting on the person (a question whose answer is not on its call yet, or a sign-in with an app that has not connected, been declined or gone) keeps the whole turn open, as does a turn that was stopped, failed, ended without words, or that a message cut across. Anything the agent said before its first step stays above the Worked row, since it answers the message at once; a thought is not a step here, so it folds and the words after it stay out. It folds when the summary arrives or `FOLD_WAIT_MS` after the turn ended, whichever is first, and a turn that does not fold gets no summary. Opened, it shows the rows it took in the order they happened, the agent's words between steps among them as they looked. A failed sign-in stays up to try again.

**A message sent during a step.** It is stored on arrival and opens the next turn with nothing under it, and says "Read after this step" (`waitsForStep`) while the step above keeps its working line. At the next point between steps the agent writes it again with `MID_TURN_NOTE`, which is what `joinedMidTurn` reads, and the turn it cut across stays open.

## The card

One card is up at a time, and all of its rules are in `cardsOf` and `workCardOf` ([work-card.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/work-card.ts)), so they can be changed in one place:

- A card stays up from one activity to the next while the new one has nothing to show, and through what the agent says between them, kept mounted, so it never blinks between them: agents narrate a line before most commands, and a card that went at each would close and open again at every step. It is drawn under the work it shows, never under words: what the agent says comes in below it, and the next activity takes it down past those words, sliding from where it was. Once the turn ends it stays until the turn folds and closes with it; in a turn that does not fold, words after the work end it. Words that are still blank or only fence marks draw no row and change nothing. A call with nothing to show changes nothing, and a call that failed has nothing to show.
- Things of one kind share a card, so a browser's commands update one page. A file card is one file: each file a call names replaces the last, and a shell call that changed several shows none.
- A task's card takes over from the work before it, and carries its own card while it works. It is the row of the call that starts the task or sends it work, keyed by that call, and comes up as the command arrives (`startsTask`), as Starting with the name the call gives, so the card that comes up is the one that goes on once the task exists. A call that ends reaching no task is a step like any other.
- A task's card stands for one stretch of the task's log ([task-slices.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/chat/task-slices.ts)). The call that started the task covers the task's first stretch, and a `task send` whose hand-off names the message it delivered (`messageId`) draws a card of its own where it was sent, covering that message up to the next one the chat sent, or the end. Its time, line and working state are its stretch's, and only the task's last stretch is ever live, so an earlier card never changes once a later send has begun; a send to a busy task settles the card above at that message, and what the step in flight writes after it (its output, its words, its error) and a work summary written after it belong to the next stretch. A stretch's time leaves out its work summary, which a separate call writes after the work. A send recorded before sends named their message draws no card, though its message still ends the stretch before it.
- A card coming up opens from nothing to its height and fades in, unless it is on screen from the first render or one went less than a second ago ([card-motion.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/transcript/card-motion.tsx)). A chat's card goes in the frame its words arrive, and closes with the rows when its turn folds; a task's card closes, since none of the task's words start below it. A page's first picture fades in over its icon.

The kinds are `page` (a browser's page, or a page or file it opened for the person), `web` (search and fetch), `files`, `images` and `generated`. A page card shows the tab the session drove last, its picture retaken after each page event and agent command. A command shows a card only for a program at the start of a shell line, so a word in its arguments or a task's assignment does not put one up. What a card shows is its face, `workCardFace` ([work-card-face.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/work-card-face.ts)): one of a fixed set of views named by kind and state (`web/opening`, `files/writing`), with its title, the note beside it and what pressing it opens, worked out from its steps and what the app knows of their paths. `WorkCardView` ([work-card.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/transcript/work-card.tsx)) draws each view by lookup, calling it as a function so a card moving between views keeps what they share.

## What a shell call did

A command's words say little about what it did, so the call records it. `watchCall` ([file-changes.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/file-changes.ts)) snapshots the chat's work folder and its folder under `Chats/` before the call, and `fileTouches` compares them after and stats every path named in the command and its output. App calls made from a script are tallied as they happen, and the pages a browser command reached are noted. It all lands as one `data-stepFacts` part ([step-facts.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/step-facts.ts)), rewritten as a running call moves and final when `settled`. The model never reads it. A number on a row (`counts`) is the agent's label for what the record counted, never the agent's own count.

## The summary line

Each time a session finishes a turn whose work folds, one call on the turn's own model at low effort writes the Worked row's line ([work-summary.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/chat/work-summary.ts)), kept as a `data-workSummary` part on the turn's last reply. It reads the person's message, the steps, what the record says the turn did, and how the reply opens. Nothing waits on it: without one the row says how long the work took. A finished task's card reads the same line, while what the agent reads of the task (`line` on its standing) stays its own last words. Activity lists the calls as Work summary.

## Reading back

A chat opens on its last turns, and `OlderTurns` asks for the page before as the reader nears the top. The scroller keeps what is on screen in place as turns arrive above it. A message the person sends is pinned with room left above it, so the last lines of what came before stay in sight (`TRANSCRIPT_PREVIOUS_TURN_PEEK`), and the move there animates (`useSlideToSent`): the scroller pins at once and a transform on its content slides from where the rows were, off the main thread. Opening a chat and every other move is a jump, and scrolling by hand ends a slide where it is. Following a turn stops when its trailing reply's first line reaches the top of the window (`useHoldAtReplyStart`), so a reply taller than the window is read from its start. Work or a card arriving after that reply resumes following unless the reader has scrolled or opened a row; a new message keeps its own send anchor. Nothing is virtualized: a chat's DOM grows only as far as someone scrolls back.

## Developer mode

Developer mode draws the same rows as everyone: `turnsOf` and the feed never ask. What only a developer reads (notes, thoughts, retried attempts, replaced replies, repeated refusals) is worked out apart from the rows by `developerNotesWithBodies` ([developer-notes.ts](../../packages/workspace/src/lib/transcript/developer-notes.ts)), each note naming the row it sits level with, which never changes as the session goes on, and the mark it wears, which a typed record gives every kind of part.

What a request went out with and how one failed being built are parts kept for a developer alone (`isDeveloperNotePart`). They are not content: `ChatReads.developerHistory` is the only read that has them, and writing one is no activity of the session's, so a message, a request and a chat's row read the same with them as without. A notice said on every request is recorded when it changes (`notesToRecord`): a rollover notice when its text does and a budget notice when its status does, so a budget card shows the count from when that status began and later counts are not recorded.

The layer that draws them ([developer-notes-layer.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/transcript/developer-notes-layer.tsx)) loads only in developer mode, draws nothing with Chat notes off (a switch in the developer strip while a chat is up, and in the command menu), and places each note with CSS anchor positioning, naming the rows from its own stylesheet by their `data-transcript-row`, so no row knows it is there. Its offsets per kind of row (`LINE_OFFSET`) are measured, and a row whose first line moves needs its number moved. A note at rest is one bare icon, thin and faint ([developer-note-marks.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/transcript/developer-note-marks.tsx), with its size and colors at the top of the stylesheet), and only an error, an amber triangle, has color. Hovering a mark steps it up and opens its card, whose heading names the note, and moving up and down swaps the card. A spot shows up to three marks and a count past four notes, an error first so a count can't stand for it, and a closed fold shows one mark or its total, with the triangle beside it when an error is in there, which hovering lists. A thought's label is only its kind, and its words are on its card. A card's words come from the walk a request makes: `SessionMessage.toUIMessages` tells a reader each note as it goes onto a user turn (`onNote`), so what a card says the model read is what the request wrote, tags included, and a part it never told says it wasn't sent.

A note stays beside what already existed when it went out: its own row if it draws one, else the nearest row before it, else the turn's message. So the first thought and the turn note of a turn sit on the person's message, and nothing that arrives later moves a note. The one exception is a row that goes away, as a retried attempt's does, whose note drops to what is before it. A part a run recorded on a step and the model reads on the next message (a step limit, an output limit, skills it wrote) stays where it was recorded, and only its card changes, from unsent to what the model read. A turn a wake opens has no message of its own, so a note before its first row sits by where the turn before it left off, and a session whose first turn opens on a wake has nothing for a note to sit beside until it has a row, so a note before that isn't drawn. The side says who it came from: what the app told the model goes on the right when it is on a user message or beside the person's message, what the agent did goes on the left. A note on a row that folds counts in the fold's total.

A card is at most 400 layout px tall and scrolls inside, with its heading and Copy staying put, and a wheel in it goes no further than the card. Its width and height give way to the window at any zoom, so it fits on screen wherever its note is.

## Beside the transcript

A chat's tabs show only in the pane beside it, under a row of them ([pane-tabs.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/window/pane-tabs.tsx)). The header's button opens and closes the pane ([pane-toggle.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/window/pane-toggle.tsx)), and the × at the end of the row closes it too. Stop sits where Send does while the box is empty and stops the chat and every task; a task's card stops that task alone. A task's tab is its title and Stop over its own transcript, reached from its card, since no screen lists a chat's tasks.

A command that outlasts its turn gets a card under the reply ([command-card.tsx](../../apps/studio/src/client/components/task/command-card.tsx)), with Stop while it runs and how long it ran once it ends. A command that ended while its turn went on gets none, since the reply covers it. The registry keeps ended commands only while the app runs, so after a restart a finished one has no card. What the chat and its tasks have running is a chip in the header, opening onto the same rows ([background-processes.md](background-processes.md)).

## Checking a change

The transcript debug page (`/debug/components/transcript`, scenarios in [`-transcript/scenarios.ts`](../../apps/studio/src/client/routes/debug/-transcript/scenarios.ts)) replays a scripted session frame by frame, with developer mode a switch away. Add a scenario for a new state rather than a test of its pixels: tests pin the rules in `turnsOf`, `work-card.ts` and `work-card-face.ts`, where they run in milliseconds.
